# Changelog

All notable user-facing changes to Tag Kit for Steam. The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), versions are unpadded CalVer (`YYYY.M.D`, with `.N` for a same-day recut), and the file is generated by [changie](https://changie.dev) from the fragments in `.changes/`.

## 2026.10.27.1 - 2026-10-27
### Added
- The settings now end in a bar of three links that stays in view on every tab, on the options page and in the userscript dialog, in English and Spanish. One reports a bug, one leaves feedback on GitHub, and one sends feedback or a bug report anonymously, with no account. The links open in a new tab, and the extension sends nothing when you use them.
### Changed
- The short description of the extension, which Chrome shows on its store page and in the browser's extension list and which the userscript carries in its header, used to stop at PvP. It now names more of what Tag Kit tags, including AI disclosure, anti-cheat, DRM, Steam Deck and Family Sharing, and ends by saying the extension collects nothing. The Spanish description was reworded to match, in Steam's own Spanish terms.

## 2026.10.27 - 2026-10-27
### Added
- You can back up your settings and your lists of ignored games, developers and publishers. Download backup on the settings page, or in the userscript's settings dialog, saves them to a JSON file on your device, and Restore from a backup reads such a file, shows its date and what it holds, and applies it only when you confirm. Restore replaces your settings and adds the file's ignored items to yours without removing any, so a file from the extension works in the userscript and the other way round. Damaged entries are left out and counted. The file never leaves your device, and lookup results are not in it.
- A package page (a store address that starts with /sub/) now sums up the tags of the games it includes, right below the package's title image and above the list of games, with one line per tag that says, in Steam's words for the tag, how many of the games carry it. It counts the games that show the tag's chip, so a tag you switched off or a game you ignored for it is left out, and a game Steam gave no answer for is not counted either way. It uses the lookups the rows already make, with no extra request, and follows your settings as you change them.
- A demo now shows the tags of its full game, in search results, on the wishlist, on sale pages such as Next Fest and in the hover card, so a demo row is no longer bare when the full game declares AI use, a kernel level anti-cheat, 3rd-party DRM and the like. The hover card and the banner on the demo's own page say "Declared for the full game" under each such tag, and the demo's own declaration for a tag, when it has one, is shown instead. These tags follow the same switches, list filters and ignore lists as any other, and ignoring the full game also covers its demos. Tag Kit looks the full games up in the same kind of batched request to Steam as every other game, once per page for the full games it has not seen, and DLC still show only their own tags. Stored lookup results from before are cleared once, so each game is checked again as you browse.
- A new tag, In-App Purchases, marks games whose Steam features list In-App Purchases, which the developer sets for the store page. The tag starts switched off; turn it on in the settings page, the toolbar popup or the userscript dialog, where it has its own badge, banner entry, list filter (Show, Dim or Hide) and color like every other tag, and ignoring a game, a developer or a publisher works for it too. Its chip reads IAP (Compras in Spanish), and the hover card and the banner use Steam's full name. A game whose features Steam does not send gets no tag either way, and a demo shows its full game's tag. Tag Kit reads it from the data it fetches for each game, with no new request. Stored lookup results from before are checked again once as you browse.
- Two new tags, Steam Deck Compatibility and Steam Machine Compatibility, show the rating Valve gives a game for each device, Verified, Playable or Unsupported. Both start switched off. Once you turn one on, its card in the settings page, the toolbar popup and the userscript dialog lets you pick which ratings to tag, Unsupported at first, and the chips read "Deck Unsupported" or "Steam Machine Playable". The badge, the banner entry (off at first) and the list filter (Show, Dim or Hide) act on the ratings you picked, and a change applies to the games on screen at once. A game Valve has not rated, such as an unreleased one, gets no tag, and a DLC shows its own rating only. The hover card ends with "According to Valve" for these tags. Tag Kit asks Steam for the ratings only while one of these tags is on, in the same batched request as the other tags, and asks once more for the games on screen when you turn one on. Stored lookup results from before are cleared once, so each game is checked again as you browse.
- A new tag, Family Sharing, reads the Family Sharing entry Valve computes in a game's Steam features. It starts switched off. Once you turn it on, its card in the settings page, the toolbar popup and the userscript dialog lets you pick which values to tag, Family Sharing or Family Sharing not Available, the second at first. A paid game whose Steam features leave Family Sharing out shows a "No Family Sharing" chip, and the hover card reads "Family Sharing is not supported for this game" and "According to the features Steam lists for this game"; the Steam Deck and Steam Machine cards keep "According to Valve". Free games, DLC, demos and games whose features Steam does not send never get that value; a demo shows its full game's tag. The badge, the banner entry (off at first) and the list filter (Show, Dim or Hide) act on the values you picked. Tag Kit reads it from the data it fetches for each game, with no new request. Stored lookup results from before are checked again once as you browse. A tag lent to a demo by Valve's data now reads "For the full game".
- You can put the tags in your own order in a new Tag order card in the General tab. Drag a tag by its handle, or click its handle and then the handle of the place it goes to, or use Space, the arrow keys and Escape. Badges on rows and images, the hover panel, the tag list on a game's page, the banner and the package summary follow your order, and a small game image shows your first two. Reset order goes back to the usual order. The tabs stay sorted by name.
- You can name a tag's chips in your own words. Each tag's tab on the options page and in the userscript dialog has a Chip text field, used on badges, in the tag list of a game's page, on large game images, on the tag's tab, in the toolbar popup and on the banner's Ignore buttons, and a shorter one for small game images, each with a counter, a Reset button and a preview of both chips as you type. Leave a field empty for the usual text. On a Steam Deck or Steam Machine chip your text replaces the device word and Steam's rating stays after it. The hover panel, the banner's headings and the package summary keep Steam's words, and a screen reader now reads every chip's visible text first, then Steam's words, also for the rating and Family Sharing chips. The color square beside a tag's hex color field now opens your browser's color picker; the hex code stays beside it and follows the pick.
### Changed
- The notice about hidden search results now looks like the store's own notice bar above the results, with the same dark ground and light yellow text, and sits right below the store's bar when it shows one. It reads, for example, "4 titles have been hidden by Tag Kit based on your settings." with an underlined Show them link, which shows those results again, dimmed, on that page only. It still names only the number and never changes your settings.
- Settings now have a tab for each tag, sorted by the tag's name and marked On or Off, beside a General tab for the language, the color help, what you ignored for all tags, and the backup. Each tag's tab holds its switches and what you ignored for that tag alone, with a count of what all tags ignore and a way to it. On a wide window the tabs form a column on the left. The page remembers the last tab, and the userscript dialog shows the same tabs, now with the language picker. The toolbar popup is now a short list of the tags, each with its Show, Dim and Hide control and its switch; a tag's name opens its tab on the options page, and a gear at the top opens all settings. Stop ignoring and Reset are now quiet text buttons.
### Fixed
- Games on a page opened while Tag Kit was pausing its lookups to Steam, such as a search results page opened from the search box's suggestions, now get their tags once the pause ends, about a minute later, instead of staying untagged until a reload. Tag Kit asks again at most twice, and the banner on a game's page fills in the same way.
- On a DLC's page, a tag list filter on Dim or Hide for the base game no longer fades or removes most of the page. The base game's tags now sit only on the small box at the top right that names the base game, and the DLC's header image, the side column, the purchase box and the description are left alone.
- The tag on a game image now sits the same distance from the top and the right edge. It was 5px from the top and flush against the right edge, and now keeps 5px from both. On a narrow image the tag still shrinks and ends in an ellipsis instead of running past the image.

## 2026.10.26 - 2026-10-26
### Added
- You can ignore a developer or a publisher. Below the entries, the banner on a game's page has a closed section, Ignore a developer or publisher, with a row for each developer and each publisher of the game and a button for each tag and one for all tags. A game is ignored for a tag when one of its developers is on your developer list or one of its publishers on your publisher list, so a studio's next release is covered at once. The settings page, the toolbar popup and the userscript dialog list them in two sections, with a Stop ignoring button. The extension reads the names from the data it already fetches for each game, to the same Valve API with no new permission, and tells a creator apart by its Steam group ID when Steam gives one and by its exact name otherwise. Stored lookup results from before are cleared once, so each game is checked again as you browse.
- On search results, when a tag's list filter is set to Hide, one line above the results now says how many results Tag Kit hid there, with a button that shows them again, dimmed, on that page only, and hides them again. The count follows new results as you scroll and any change to your settings or ignored games, and the button never changes your settings; reloading the page hides the results again.
### Changed
- Ignoring a game for a tag, or for all tags, now ignores its DLC for that tag too, on the game's own page, in search, on the wishlist and on sale pages. The extension takes the link from the base game that Steam already names for each DLC in the data it fetches, so a DLC released later follows at once and nothing new is requested or stored in your list of ignored games. Stop ignoring the game and its DLC come back. Stored lookup results from before are cleared once, so each game is checked again as you browse.
### Fixed
- On a package page (a store address that starts with /sub/), the tag badge now sits on the included game in "Items included in this package" instead of on the package's title image at the top, and a dimmed tag list filter dims only that game's row instead of the whole content column.

## 2026.10.25 - 2026-10-25
### Added
- The extension reads six more store declarations from the data it already fetches for each game. They are kernel-level anti-cheat, other anti-cheat, third-party DRM, a required third-party account, a third-party EULA and PvP modes. Each game shows one short chip per kind, such as KLAC or DRM, and a +N chip stands for the kinds that do not fit. A card on hover or keyboard focus lists every kind in Steam's own words and ends with a line that names the developer as the source. The banner on a game's page lists every kind the developer declared as one line each, in Steam's words, with its own Ignore button, and keeps the developer's full text for AI. The PvP tag starts switched off and you can turn it on in the settings. The lookup is the one the extension already made, to the same Valve API with no new permission, and a game's own page now makes it too for a game not stored yet. Stored lookup results from before lack these kinds, so those games are checked again as you browse.
- Each tag has its own settings section with a switch for the tag, its badge, its entry in the banner and a list filter (Show, Dim or Hide) for the games that carry it. When one game carries several tags, the strongest filter wins. You can ignore a game for one tag or for all tags from the banner, and the list of ignored games in settings shows each game under the scope you ignored it for, each with its own Stop ignoring button.
- Each tag's settings section can be closed and opened again, in the toolbar popup, on the settings page and in the userscript dialog. A closed section still shows the tag's name and whether the tag is on or off, and the extension remembers which sections you closed.

### Changed
- The extension is now called Tag Kit for Steam (short name Tag Kit; in Spanish, Tag Kit para Steam). Its description lists the tags it covers, and its icon is a Steam badge with a tag at the lower right. The userscript keeps its earlier script name for now.
- Settings and the list of ignored games now live in a per-tag layout, so more kinds of store declarations can be added later. The first start after the update copies your badge, banner, list filter and language settings and your ignored games to the AI tag, and clears the stored lookup results once, so each game is checked again as you browse. Your old settings stay in storage as they were.
- The AI badge now reads AI, and its card and the notice on a game's page carry Steam's own heading for the disclosure, "AI Generated Content Disclosure", in English and in Steam's Spain Spanish wording. They replace the label "AI use reported". The card ends with a line that names the developer as the source.
- A game's own store page now lists its Tag Kit tags in a section of their own, right below Steam's popular user-defined tags and headed "Tag Kit tags for this product". It shows one short chip for every tag the game declares, such as AI, KLAC or DRM, wrapping onto more rows, and a chip leaves out only that tag when you switch its badge off or ignore the game for it. Hovering a chip, or tabbing to it, opens the card with the tag in Steam's words. The lone AI chip under Developer and Publisher is gone, and a game with nothing to show gets no section.
- The build folders and release zips are named after the browser engines, `chromium` and `gecko`, instead of Chrome and Firefox; the Chrome Web Store and Firefox Add-ons listings keep their names.
- The short chip for kernel level anti-cheat reads KLAC in English instead of Kernel AC. Its card and the banner keep Steam's own line, "Uses Kernel Level Anti-Cheat".

## 2026.10.22 - 2026-10-22
### Added
- The extension now speaks Spanish. Badges, the banner, the options page, the toolbar popup and the userscript dialog follow the store page language (Spanish and Latin American Spanish), and the language picker on the options page lists it. The Spanish store name is "Marcador de IA para Steam".
### Changed
- Every label for a game whose developer reported AI use now reads "AI use reported": the badge in search results and lists, the badge on game images, the hover panel, the chip in the game's summary box, and the banner heading on its store page. The chip stands alone under Developer and Publisher, lined up with their names. Settings and the toolbar popup use the same wording.
- The per-game opt-out button now reads "Ignore this game", the list of those games in settings reads "Games you ignored", and its button reads "Stop ignoring". Steam has its own Ignore button; this one only affects this extension.
### Fixed
- On a game's page, the badge on a DLC row now follows the DLC's name instead of floating between the name and the price, and the "Ignore this game" button matches the size and edge of Steam's own buttons below it. Hovering a badge in search results no longer opens Steam's game popup beside the disclosure panel. On the options page, the Show, Dim and Hide control fits its labels instead of spanning the card, and help text is larger.
- The settings page shows the extension icon on its browser tab instead of the generic puzzle piece.

## 2026.10.21 - 2026-10-21
### Added
- Browser extension for Chrome and Firefox. On the Steam store, games whose developers disclose generative AI use get a "AI use disclosed" badge in search results and lists, and their store page shows the developer's disclosure in their own words. Lists can show, dim or hide these games, and you can stop marking a game you have checked. Settings live in the toolbar popup and the options page. Nothing is collected; game lookups go to Valve's own servers without your cookies.
- Userscript for Tampermonkey and Violentmonkey, with the same badges, banner and list filter as the extension. Its settings open from the script manager's menu in a small dialog. Settings and results stay in the manager's own storage, and game lookups go to Valve's own servers without your cookies.
### Changed
- Badges now appear on Next Fest and other sale pages, category hubs, developer and publisher pages, the personal calendar, the discovery queue preview, and on home page tabs you open after the page loads. Store cards whose picture arrives a moment after the rest of the card get their badge too, wishlist rows that load during a fast scroll keep their wishlist badge, and a carousel slide that switches to another game drops the previous game's badge.
- The toolbar popup is more compact. Its sections are flat, with thin dividers instead of cards, small captions, tighter rows and a slimmer Show, Dim and Hide control. An empty list of games you stopped marking is left out of the popup. The options page uses tighter spacing too.
- Hovering or tabbing to a badge or to the summary row on a game's page now opens a small panel, styled like Steam's own game hover, with the developer's AI disclosure in their own words and the AI service they named. It replaces the plain browser tooltip, which cut the text at 200 characters.
### Fixed
- A badge no longer disappears from a game card after Steam's own hover popup for that card closes.
