# RQ1: What does Steam record per kind, and who declares it?

Researched 2026-10-24. Tags: `verified from source` means the cited page or API response was read and says it; `search-summary` means the claim comes only from a web search result summary; `unverified` means a single weak source or an inference. Web fetches went through a small summarizing model, so API values read that way are "seen once through a helper" even when tagged verified; the local fixtures under tests/fixtures are first-hand captures from 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-21. The Valve announcement of 2024-10-30 and all Steamworks partner pages behind a login were unreachable, so every statement about what that post says is a press report.

## Answer in short

- Steam stores all four kinds in one object, `extra_details` of IStoreBrowseService/GetItems (proto `StoreItem_ExtraDetails`): `anticheat` (object), `drm_third_party_type` and `drm_activation_limit` (strings), `user_account_third_party` (string) with `user_account_third_party_link_to_steam` (bool), and `eula` (list of name plus url). Every field is optional, so a missing key means "not recorded", never "none". There is no field for a required third-party launcher; it shows up inside the DRM or account free text.
- Anti-cheat is the only kind with a public Valve rule: Steamworks post "New Tool for Describing Anti-Cheat in Games", 2024-10-30. Press reports it as mandatory for new submissions that install a client-side kernel-mode anti-cheat, optional for every other anti-cheat, with Valve contacting partners of existing games. The Steamworks Store Page doc lists an "Anti-Cheat Declaration" under the Basic Info tab.
- For third-party DRM, third-party account and EULA no Valve announcement, rule text or start date was found in public sources. The values are free text typed by the publisher (for example "Denuvo", "Denuvo Anti-tamper" and "Denuvo Anti-Tampering" for the same vendor).
- All four are self-reported. No source says Valve inspects binaries. Press says Valve checks that anti-cheat notices are present and correct; no penalty or enforcement action was found for any kind. As far as public sources show, developers can omit DRM, account and EULA data, and non-kernel anti-cheat; records with an absent key are common, but whether each such game really has the feature was not checked.

## Findings

### 1. Anti-cheat: what developers must or may declare, and since when

Date and status of the primary source.

- Valve's Steamworks Development post "New Tool for Describing Anti-Cheat in Games" is dated 2024-10-30 by press (source: https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/10/steam-games-will-now-need-to-fully-disclose-kernel-level-anti-cheat-on-store-pages/), and a Slashdot story of the same day carries the date in its path (source: https://tech.slashdot.org/story/24/10/30/212206/, search result only). `verified from source` for the date via press.
- The post body did not load in five attempts: steamcommunity.com returned only the title and site navigation, the `?l=english` variant the same, store.steampowered.com/news/group/4145017/view/4547038620960934857 the same, the Steamworks group RSS lists only ten recent posts without it, and the Wayback Machine is blocked in this environment (source: https://steamcommunity.com/groups/steamworks/announcements/detail/4547038620960934857). `unverified` against the original.

What press reports Valve wrote.

- Valve's quoted lines: developers are "looking for the right way to share anti-cheat information"; players "have been requesting more transparency around the anti-cheat services used in games"; "we generally think that any game that makes use of anti-cheat technology would benefit from letting players know" (source: https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/10/steam-games-will-now-need-to-fully-disclose-kernel-level-anti-cheat-on-store-pages/). `verified from source` (press quotes). A second read of the same page found no quoted Valve text for the mandatory rule, the existing-games rule or the accuracy checks, so those are the article author's summary there.
- Mandatory: kernel-level anti-cheat must be declared. AlternativeTo says the field is mandatory for new game submissions whose game installs a client-side, kernel-mode anti-cheat (source: https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/10/steam-will-now-require-developers-to-disclose-kernel-mode-anti-cheat-on-store-pages/). GIGAZINE says disclosure is mandatory for new games with kernel-level anti-cheat (source: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241031-steam-tool-describing-anti-cheat). PC World says such games "now have to declare" it (source: https://www.pcworld.com/article/2507080/steam-will-show-when-games-use-kernel-level-anti-cheat-on-store-pages.html). Three press reads agree; `verified from source` for the press, original post unread.
- Optional: client-side or server-side anti-cheat that is not kernel-level is optional, though Valve encourages it (source: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241031-steam-tool-describing-anti-cheat). PC World reports server-side systems that use no separate client software are exempt (source: https://www.pcworld.com/article/2507080/steam-will-show-when-games-use-kernel-level-anti-cheat-on-store-pages.html). The exemption is stated once and is `unverified` elsewhere.
- Form: a new field in the "Edit Store Page" section where the developer specifies the anti-cheat software (source: https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/10/steam-will-now-require-developers-to-disclose-kernel-mode-anti-cheat-on-store-pages/). GIGAZINE adds a pre-populated list of several tools and a custom entry for tools not on the list, and describes one checkbox for kernel-level tools (source: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241031-steam-tool-describing-anti-cheat). A search summary of other press describes two checkboxes, kernel-level integration and whether users must remove residual files after uninstall (source: web search summary of the Neowin, Tom's Hardware and Techspot items, none readable: Neowin returned 403 and Tom's Hardware returned only its headline). The second checkbox is `search-summary`; the stored fields `kernel_mode` and `uninstall_completely` (section 3) match it.
- Existing games: GIGAZINE quotes Valve as saying it will "review existing games and contact partners whose games fall into this category" (source: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241031-steam-tool-describing-anti-cheat). AlternativeTo says Valve is contacting partners with existing games that use such software (source: https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/10/steam-will-now-require-developers-to-disclose-kernel-mode-anti-cheat-on-store-pages/). Press differs on scope: a first read of GamingOnLinux summarized "new and existing games", a second read of the same article found no such sentence. No deadline for existing games appeared anywhere. Net: mandatory for new submissions from 2024-10-30; for existing games enforced by Valve outreach, timing unknown.
- Steamworks docs: the Store Page doc lists "Anti-Cheat Declaration - Share anti-cheat information about your game" under the "Basic Info" tab, with a link to more info that sits behind the partner site (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/page). `verified from source`. The doc "Anti-cheat and Game Bans" tells developers to warn customers that an anti-cheat system exists and has nothing on the store-page declaration (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/anticheat). `verified from source`.

### 2. Third-party DRM, third-party account and EULA: what developers must or may declare

- No public Valve rule found. The Store Page doc's Basic Info tab lists accessibility support, controller support, social media links and the anti-cheat declaration; nothing on DRM, third-party accounts, EULAs or legal information (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/page). `verified from source`. The Steam DRM doc covers the Steam wrapper and using it with other DRM, and has no store-page disclosure text and no mention of Denuvo (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/drm). `verified from source`. The Steamworks doc index has no entry for EULA, launcher or release checklist (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc). `verified from source` (navigation only; the Steam Distribution Agreement, review process pages and release checklist sit behind login and were not read).
- A search for a Steamworks doc on adding a EULA found only Steam-hosted publisher EULA pages at store.steampowered.com/eula/ and the Workshop EULA call in ISteamUGC; no developer instruction page (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/api/ISteamUGC). `search-summary`.
- Community claims, none from Valve. A forum commenter, thread not identified, says Steamworks gives publishers two flags, "3rd-Party Account" and "3rd-Party DRM", and that nobody forces them to set them (source: web search summary). `unverified`; the "Requires 3rd-Party Account" thread, read for 15 of its 20 replies, does not contain it (source: https://steamcommunity.com/discussions/forum/10/601901188455074095/). A 2018 thread's top reply says publishers are not obliged to state third-party DRM (source: https://steamcommunity.com/discussions/forum/1/1736595227852911346, search summary). `unverified`. A Steam group says Valve policy is to disclose DRM on relevant store pages, with inconsistent results (source: https://steamcommunity.com/groups/nodrm, search summary). `unverified`. A forum remark that the Steam Distribution Agreement sits under NDA means its terms on these notices cannot be checked (source: https://steamcommunity.com/discussions/forum/0/1755779919109939706, search summary). `unverified`.
- Start dates: none found for any of the three. A 2014-era forum thread says older games list such requirements in the system requirements text because they predate when listing third-party accounts or DRM was required; that is a user inference and the thread was not pinned down (source: web search summary, no stable URL). `unverified`. The notices exist on pages today (see section 4).
- Who writes the text: a forum commenter says everything on a store page comes from the developer or publisher, not Valve (source: https://steamcommunity.com/discussions/forum/1/1736595227852911346, search summary). `unverified`; consistent with the free-text values in section 3.

### 3. Stored fields and what they mean

Where the data lives.

- `StoreItem_ExtraDetails` declares `drm_third_party_type` (string, 5), `drm_activation_limit` (string, 6), `user_account_third_party` (string, 7), `user_account_third_party_link_to_steam` (bool, 8), `anticheat` (`StoreItem_ExtraDetails_Anticheat`, 9) and repeated `eula` (`StoreItem_ExtraDetails_Eula`, 10) (source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SteamDatabase/Protobufs/master/webui/common.proto, read at offset 200000). `verified from source`. Standalone `StoreItem_Anticheat` and `StoreItem_Eula` messages with the same fields are referenced in comments, which suggests a refactor. DRM and account data are plain fields on the parent, not nested messages.
- `StoreItem_ExtraDetails_Anticheat`: `kernel_mode` bool 1, `uninstall_completely` bool 2, `boot_protection` bool 3, `boot_protection_name` string 4, `name` string 5, `name_loc` string 6. `StoreItem_ExtraDetails_Eula`: `name` string 1, `url` string 2 (same source). `verified from source`.
- The request flag is `include_extra_details` in `data_request`; the live JSON uses the same key names. 25 apps were sampled on 2026-10-24 plus 7 in the 2026-10-20 fixture (source: https://api.steampowered.com/IStoreBrowseService/GetItems/v1 with `input_json` `{"ids":[{"appid":N}],"context":{"language":"english","country_code":"US"},"data_request":{"include_extra_details":true}}`; local fixture tests/fixtures/getitems/mixed-states.json). `verified from source`. In the sections below, "GetItems v1 URL" means this call.

anticheat.kernel_mode and uninstall_completely.

- `kernel_mode: true` is the "kernel-level" checkbox. Seen true for Call of Duty (1938090), Battlefield 2042 (1517290), HELLDIVERS 2 (553850), Delta Force (2507950), Destiny 2 (1085660), Apex Legends (1172470), Grand Theft Auto V (271590) and ARC Raiders (1808500) (source: GetItems v1 URL; fixture mixed-states.json). `verified from source`.
- `uninstall_completely: true` is the second checkbox, read from the field name as "the anti-cheat is removed on uninstall"; the key is absent when not set. The ARC Raiders record has no `uninstall_completely`, and its page appends " - Requires manual removal after game uninstall" (source: tests/fixtures/app-page/1808500-ai-only.html lines 70-72). The Call of Duty record has it true and the live page block lists no removal line (source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1938090/?l=english). `verified from source` for both observations. The polarity (true means clean uninstall, so the warning shows when the key is false or unset) is inferred from these two apps: `unverified` as a rule. The press describes the second checkbox the other way round ("users need to remove any residual files"), and the Steamworks UI label was not seen.
- False booleans appear to be omitted: Counter-Strike 2 (730) has `{"name":"VAC (Valve Anti-Cheat)","name_loc":"#Store_AntiCheat_Type_vac"}` with no `kernel_mode` (source: GetItems v1 URL). So "unset" and "false" cannot be told apart in the JSON. `verified from source`.
- Empty and bare objects occur: Marvel Rivals (2767030) returned `anticheat: {}`; Battlefield 2042 returned `{"kernel_mode":true,"uninstall_completely":true}` with no name (source: GetItems v1 URL). `verified from source` (one read each). An empty object is an unexplained state; do not read it as "no anti-cheat".

anticheat.name versus name_loc.

- `name` is the display string. When the developer picked an entry from Valve's list, `name_loc` holds a localization token of the form `#Store_AntiCheat_Type_<id>`: `denuvo` (ARC Raiders, name "Denuvo"), `easyac` ("Easy Anti-Cheat"), `battleye` ("BattlEye"), `ace` ("Anti-Cheat Expert (ACE)"), `vac` ("VAC (Valve Anti-Cheat)") (source: GetItems v1 URL; fixture mixed-states.json). `verified from source`.
- The API does not resolve the token: with `language: german` and `country_code: DE` HELLDIVERS 2 and Delta Force returned the same English `name` and the same token (source: GetItems v1 URL, german request). `verified from source` (one read). Whether the store page localizes the name through the token was not tested.
- Records exist with neither `name` nor `name_loc` (Call of Duty, Battlefield 2042). No record with a `name` but no `name_loc` was found, so the claim that a custom entry has no token is `unverified`. The press says a custom entry is allowed (GIGAZINE, section 1).
- "Denuvo" in `anticheat.name` is the vendor's anti-cheat product. The DRM field uses the same vendor name for a different product. Treat them as separate facts.
- Open discrepancy: the Call of Duty store page lists "RICOCHET Anti-Cheat" as a notice line, but its GetItems record (read twice on 2026-10-24 and once in the 2026-10-20 fixture) has no `name`. The page may take that line from another source (source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1938090/?l=english). `unverified` as to where it comes from.

anticheat.boot_protection and boot_protection_name.

- `boot_protection: true` with `boot_protection_name: "secureboot_tpm2"` was seen only on Call of Duty (1938090) (source: GetItems v1 URL; fixture german-context.json). `boot_protection_name` is a key, not display text: the page renders "Boot Protection - Requires both Secure Boot & TPM 2.0" and, when the visitor's PC lacks them, a warning box "Secure Boot & TPM 2.0 Not Detected" saying both "must be enabled to play some or all parts of this game" (source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1938090/?l=english). `verified from source` (two page reads, one app). Other key values are unknown; only one value was seen.

drm_third_party_type and drm_activation_limit.

- `drm_third_party_type` is free text typed by the publisher. Observed: "Denuvo Anti-Tampering" (Hogwarts Legacy 990080), "Denuvo" (Black Myth: Wukong 2358720), "Denuvo Anti-tamper" (Metaphor: ReFantazio 2679460, Street Fighter 6 1364780, Assassin's Creed Shadows 3159330), "EA online activation and EA app software installation and background use required." (Star Wars Jedi: Survivor 1774580) and "EA online activation and EA app software installation and background use required. Denuvo Anti-tamper" (EA Sports FC 25 2669320) (source: GetItems v1 URL). `verified from source` (one read). The field is a mix of vendor name and prose, so values are not an enum; a required third-party launcher is expressed here (the EA app) or in the account field, not in a dedicated field.
- `drm_activation_limit` (string) was absent in every record sampled. The Street Fighter 6 page nonetheless shows "5 different PC within a day machine activation limit" beside its DRM notice (source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1364780/?l=english). `unverified` as to which field feeds that line.

user_account_third_party and user_account_third_party_link_to_steam.

- `user_account_third_party` is the free-text name of the required account: "Activision Account" (Call of Duty), "Rockstar Games" (Red Dead Redemption 2 1174180, Grand Theft Auto V), "PlayStation Network" (HELLDIVERS 2), "Xbox Live" (Forza Horizon 5 1551360), "Capcom ID" (Street Fighter 6), "Ubisoft Account" (Assassin's Creed Shadows), "EA Account" (Jedi: Survivor, FC 25), "LEVEL INFINITE PASS" (Delta Force) (source: GetItems v1 URL and fixture mixed-states.json). `verified from source`.
- `user_account_third_party_link_to_steam: true` means the third-party account can be linked to the Steam account. The page renders it as a parenthetical: "Requires 3rd-Party Account: Activision Account (Supports Linking to Steam Account)" and "Requires 3rd-Party Account: Capcom ID (Supports Linking to Steam Account)" (source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1938090/?l=english and https://store.steampowered.com/app/1364780/?l=english). `verified from source`. When the flag is absent (EA Account on Jedi: Survivor and FC 25) the page wording was not fetched; that it then omits the parenthetical is `unverified`.

eula.

- `eula[]` is a list of `{name, url}` objects, one per EULA, for example `{"name":"Resident Evil Village EULA","url":"https://store.steampowered.com//eula/1196590_eula_1"}` (source: GetItems v1 URL). `verified from source`. The URL pattern is `store.steampowered.com//eula/<appid>_eula_<n>` (double slash as served; n was 0 or 1 in the sample), so the EULA page is hosted on Steam. The ARC Raiders EULA page is a Steam-hosted shell that links out to the publisher's terms (source: https://store.steampowered.com/eula/1808500_eula_0). `verified from source` (one read).
- The field says the game shows a EULA to accept; it does not mark whether the EULA is "third-party" versus the developer's own. The page wording is fixed: "Requires agreement to a 3rd-party EULA" (section 4). Resident Evil Village, Resident Evil 4, Tekken 8 and Dragon Age: The Veilguard each carry an `eula` entry but no account or DRM, so the EULA is declared independently of the other kinds. `verified from source`.

### 4. Store-page wording per kind

- Anti-cheat block (ARC Raiders fixture): a div with classes `anticheat_section DRM_notice` holding "Uses Kernel Level Anti-Cheat", then a div `anticheat_name` with the name and a span `anticheat_uninstalls` reading " - Requires manual removal after game uninstall" (source: tests/fixtures/app-page/1808500-ai-only.html lines 70-73). `verified from source`. The class `DRM_notice` is shared by every notice, including anti-cheat.
- EULA block: a `DRM_notice` div with `role="button"` holding "Requires agreement to a 3rd-party EULA" and a link whose text is the `eula[].name` (same fixture lines 74-77). `verified from source`.
- Third-party account line: "Requires 3rd-Party Account: <name> (Supports Linking to Steam Account)" (source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1364780/?l=english). `verified from source`.
- DRM line: "Incorporates 3rd-party DRM: Denuvo Anti-tamper" (source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1364780/?l=english). `verified from source` (one read); an earlier search summary paraphrased it as "Includes", so exact verb wording needs a raw HTML check. The Call of Duty page had no DRM notice and its record has no DRM field (source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1938090/?l=english).
- Boot protection: "Boot Protection - Requires both Secure Boot & TPM 2.0" (source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1938090/?l=english). `verified from source`.
- The notices use English text only in the captures; other-language wording was not checked here (RQ4 covers it).

### 5. Self-reported or verified, enforcement, old games, omission

- Self-reported: the data lives in the developer's Steamworks store page configuration, and the free-text, publisher-specific values (section 3) are consistent with developer entry. No public source describes Valve detecting kernel drivers, Denuvo wrapping, account walls or EULAs on its own. For anti-cheat the Steamworks doc names the declaration as something the developer shares (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/page). `verified from source` for the doc line; the absence of any verification statement is a negative finding, not proof.
- Valve checking: press says new games will be checked so that the notices are present and accurate (source: web search summary of PC World, GamingOnLinux and others; GamingOnLinux's second read did not confirm it). `search-summary`. No source says how, and none describes an audit result.
- Penalties: none found for any kind. The public Steamworks pages read contain no enforcement language for these declarations (sources: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/page, https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/anticheat, https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/drm). `verified from source` for the absence on those three pages; the partner review pages and Distribution Agreement were not reachable.
- Old games: for anti-cheat, Valve contacts partners of existing games (section 1). Live data shows old apps do carry declarations: Grand Theft Auto V (appid 271590, a legacy listing) has `anticheat` `{"kernel_mode":true,"name":"BattlEye","name_loc":"#Store_AntiCheat_Type_battleye"}` and an account entry (source: GetItems v1 URL). `verified from source`; when each record was added is unknown. Developers can edit the store page themselves after release and changes go live on publish (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/page). `verified from source`. No retroactive sweep was found for DRM, account or EULA.
- Omission: as far as public sources show, developers may omit DRM, account and EULA data. A Steam forum report describes a 2024 case where a game's third-party DRM info was missing at first and was added to the requirements text after launch (source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/2291060/discussions/0/4509876314624602448, search summary). `search-summary`. Records with an absent key include apps often reported to use the feature, but this was not checked against each game, so it is only a pointer: Elden Ring (1245620), NARAKA: BLADEPOINT (1203220) and Black Desert (582660) have no `anticheat`; Dragon Age: The Veilguard (1845910) has an EULA but no account or DRM entry while the other EA titles sampled have EA entries (source: GetItems v1 URL). `unverified` as evidence of omission; whether each game ships kernel-mode anti-cheat or needs an EA account was not verified.
- Optional declarations do occur: Counter-Strike 2 declares VAC with no kernel flag, and Valve is its developer (source: GetItems v1 URL). `verified from source`.

### 6. What stayed unreachable or unanswered

- The original Steamworks announcement text (all fetch routes returned navigation only); the exact field labels and checkbox wording in the Steamworks UI; the "more info" link behind "Anti-Cheat Declaration".
- Any Valve statement on DRM, third-party account or EULA declaration rules, their start dates, and whether the Steamworks admin pages make them mandatory; the Steam Distribution Agreement, review guidelines and release checklist (login required).
- Whether Valve audits any declaration and any penalty applied; whether `drm_activation_limit` is ever populated; whether a custom anti-cheat name lacks `name_loc`; localized page wording of the notices.

## Caveats and version notes

- Data values are a 2026-10-24 snapshot of live records, read through a summarizing helper; the 2026-10-20 fixtures agree for the apps they cover (1808500, 1938090), and the fixture files are the byte-exact source for those two. Records change when developers edit store pages. The question of what changed around 2026-10-22 belongs to RQ3 and is not covered here.
- Press accounts of the 2024-10-30 post differ on whether existing games were covered, and on one versus two checkboxes. Treat "mandatory for new submissions with kernel-mode client anti-cheat" as the stable core.
- `drm_third_party_type` and `user_account_third_party` are free text, so any classification (for example Denuvo or not) needs string handling with spelling variants; `name_loc` tokens are the only stable anti-cheat identifiers and cover only list entries.
- Two page-versus-API gaps are unexplained: the RICOCHET line on the Call of Duty page and the activation-limit line on the Street Fighter 6 page. A page-only notice may exist that GetItems does not carry.
- The Call of Duty and Street Fighter 6 page reads came from the helper's markdown view of the full page; the helper capped quotes at 125 characters, so surrounding text was described, not quoted.
- Search results for forum claims were summaries and several were not pinned to a single thread; each is tagged accordingly.
