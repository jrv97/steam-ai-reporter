# RQ3: How complete and reliable is Steam's AI content disclosure as a signal?

Researched 2026-10-20. All web fetches were summarised by a small model; where a page could not be fetched (403, truncated) the claim is tagged `search-summary` or `unverified`. No analysis of Steam AI disclosure by Ian Cheng was found; the Totally Human analyses are by Ichiro Lambe (see sources).

## Answer in short

- The disclosure is a self-reported, unaudited floor: it is a lower bound on AI in shipped content, and Valve's January 2026 rewrite exempts AI dev tools ("efficiency gains"). The signal can never mean "this game used no AI".
- Scale: about 1,000 disclosing games (Apr 2024), 7,818 (Jul 2025, ~7%), 10,258 (Nov 2025, ~8%), more than 15,000 games and software on SteamDB (Feb 2026), ~9,400 flagged in a 53,597-release census (Jul 2026); share of new releases 10.9% (2024), 19.9% (2025), 30.8% (2026 to date).
- Concrete gaps exist and are named: The Alters (no disclosure), Crimson Desert (disclosure added after launch), Black Ops 6 (disclosure about a year after launch), retroactive flags (22% of significant-revenue AI games, per one census), vague wording; no case was found of Valve delisting an existing game for non-disclosure.
- Debate: Tim Sweeney called labels senseless (X post, late Nov 2025) and "really irresponsible of Valve" (PC Gamer interview, reported 25 Jun 2026); no Valve response found; as of 2026-10-20 the requirement is still in force, last rewritten in January 2026 (narrower and still in force), and no native Steam filter or tag exists as of the latest coverage found (June 2026).
- For the extension: expect many false negatives (exempt tooling, late or missing disclosures, strategic omission) and some false positives (minor, peripheral or since-removed use; negated text such as "we do NOT use AI-generated art"); label it "discloses AI" and avoid "uses AI" and "AI-free"; community signals (review text, SteamDB tag) are noisy and mostly derived from the same disclosure.

## Findings

### 1. How many Steam games disclose AI, and the trend 2024 to 2026

Timeline of counts (each row has a different scope and method; rows are not strictly comparable):

- 2024-01-10: Valve adds an AI disclosure section to the Steamworks Content Survey, split into pre-generated and live-generated, and says it will show much of it on Steam pages (source: https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/steam-pages-will-now-disclose-which-games-use-ai-generated-content/).
- 2024-04-24: Totally Human media (Ichiro Lambe) ran a script over the store "about" sections and found 1,000 games with a genai disclosure, about ten times what he expected (source: https://totallyhuman.io/blog/the-surprising-number-of-steam-games-that-use-genai). The 2025 post puts this at about 1.1% of the library (source: https://totallyhuman.io/blog/the-surprising-new-number-of-genai-games-on-steam).
- 2025-02-25: SteamDB launches an automatic "AI Content Disclosed" user tag derived from store-page disclosures (source: https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/02/steamdb-now-lets-you-filter-out-steam-games-with-ai-generation/; SteamDB's own announcement: https://x.com/SteamDB/status/1894080893632884820, seen only as a search result title). A ">4,900 games at launch" figure appeared in a search summary only: unverified.
- 2025-07-13: 7,818 of about 114,126 games (~7%) disclose; a little under 20% of 2025 releases; ~60% of disclosures are visual asset generation; counts come from Steam's API via the author's "We Love Every Game" platform (source: https://totallyhuman.io/blog/the-surprising-new-number-of-genai-games-on-steam). VGC reported this on 2025-07-17 as "up 800%" from about 1,000 a year earlier (source: https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/steam-games-disclosing-generative-ai-use-are-up-800-this-year/).
- 2025-10-21: 12,284 games carry a disclosure per SteamDB (source: https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/10/want-to-avoid-ai-gen-on-steam-this-browser-userscript-might-save-your-day/). The same month's Next Fest: 504 demos, 17% of the event, per a PCGamesN article dated 2025-10-21 (source: https://www.pcgamesn.com/steam/next-fest-generative-ai; the fetch summary attributed the tally to techraptor; attribution is unclear).
- 2025-11-06: Totally Human counts 10,258 titles (~8% of Steam), estimated combined gross about $660 million (Boxleiter estimate, described by the author as the roughest calculation) (source: https://totallyhuman.io/blog/games-with-ai-disclosures-have-grossed-an-estimated-660m-on-steam). The gap to SteamDB's 12,284 two weeks earlier is plausibly scope (SteamDB counts "games and software"), but no source states the reason: unverified.
- 2026-02-12: "more than 15,000 games and software" carry the label per a SteamDB search; Steam itself publishes no total (source: https://stuff.co.za/2026/02/12/are-video-game-developers-using-ai-players-want-to-know-but-the-rules-are-patchy/, a republication of a piece from The Conversation by Thomas Byers and Bjorn Nansen).
- 2026-06-15/16 (June Next Fest): 1,694 of 8,682 event items carry the AI disclosure tag on SteamDB (~19.5%), per a ResetEra thread linking the SteamDB lists (source: https://www.resetera.com/threads/steam-next-fest-has-over-8-600-demos-and-almost-1700-of-them-have-ai-disclosures.1551832/); Eurogamer's count of 1,704 of 8,700 (19.5%) was relayed by Engadget on 2026-06-16 (source: https://engadget.com/2195840/around-a-fifth-of-steam-next-fest-demos-have-a-generative-ai-disclosure). The 10-vs-9 difference is probably snapshot timing; unverified. PCGamesN found 10 of the top 100 demos by popularity carry a disclosure, per a secondary aggregator (source: https://letsdatascience.com/news/over-1000-steam-next-fest-games-disclose-generative-ai-6c11ab90). A PC Gamer figure of 551 of 4,397 SteamDB products was seen only in a search summary and uses a different list: unverified.
- 2026-07-13: Sulka Haro's census (Substack "fragwyz") of 53,597 "Games" releases from mid-2023 to mid-2026 found about 9,400 AI-flagged; roughly one in three new games flagged by mid-2026; flagged launches rose from about 13 per month before the mandate to about 530; 60 to 90 percent of growth in monthly releases is AI-flagged; the 50% crossing in 2027 or 2028 is a scenario and no forecast (source: https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/three-years-of-ai-on-steam). Per-year shares of 10.9% (2024), 19.9% (2025) and 30.8% (2026 to date) are given by secondary coverage (source: https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-07-20-steam-ai-disclosure-study); the Substack fetch said yearly figures are approximations read from monthly charts; treat them as +/- a few points. PC Gamer covered the census (Shaun Prescott's "Steam week in review" column; page itself not readable, headline seen in search results: https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-take-cover-because-it-looks-like-more-than-half-of-steam-games-will-have-an-ai-disclosure-by-2027-2028/).
- Method note from Haro: the census enumerates Steam search results and checks each store page for the "AI Generated Content Disclosure" section; flag timing comes from Wayback snapshots (source: https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/three-years-of-ai-on-steam).

Trend in one line: roughly 1% of the catalog in April 2024, 7% in July 2025, 8% in November 2025; among new releases about 7% at the February 2024 start, about 20% in 2025, about 30% in 2026 (sources as above). The flag is binary and does not measure how much AI was used (source: https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/three-years-of-ai-on-steam).

Valve statements: Valve publishes no count. What it has said is in the Steamworks Content Survey docs (AI content section: pre-generated and live-generated; "efficiency gains through the use of these tools is not the focus of this section"; live-generated must describe guardrails; live-generated AI adult only sexual content not wanted "at this time") (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey). No Valve comment on the Next Fest figures appeared in the engadget piece (source: https://engadget.com/2195840/around-a-fifth-of-steam-next-fest-demos-have-a-generative-ai-disclosure).

### 2. Known gaps, with named examples

Structural gaps in the rules

- January 2026 rewrite (reported 2026-01-16 to 2026-01-20): the form now covers AI content "consumed by players" (art, audio, localization, narrative, marketing, store page, community assets); AI-powered dev tools such as code helpers need no disclosure; examples given are using ChatGPT to check code or an image generator to brainstorm concept art if output does not reach players (sources: https://80.lv/articles/steam-has-clarified-its-rules-on-the-use-of-ai-in-video-games, https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use, https://gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-tweaks-and-clarifies-ai-disclosure-rules-for-steam). 80.lv calls the change possibly "largely cosmetic" because Valve likely never enforced dev-only disclosure (source: https://80.lv/articles/steam-has-clarified-its-rules-on-the-use-of-ai-in-video-games). Gamingbible's author objects that concept art stages go undisclosed (source: https://www.gamingbible.com/news/platform/steam/steam-ai-changes-disclosure-rules-203740-20260120).
- Free-form text with no official tag leaves shoppers unable to search or filter; it is "not clear what happens" when a developer fails to disclose (source: https://stuff.co.za/2026/02/12/are-video-game-developers-using-ai-players-want-to-know-but-the-rules-are-patchy/). The fetched Steamworks page has no AI-specific enforcement or removal language, and says survey answers changed after approval require contacting Steam support (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey).
- The in-game overlay report that Valve announced in 2024 is for live-generated content that guardrails should have caught; it does not cover reporting undisclosed AI (sources: https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/steam-pages-will-now-disclose-which-games-use-ai-generated-content/, https://dashfight.com/news/steam-adds-an-option-to-report-ai-content-4901).
- Placement: the section sits at the bottom of the store page in the same format as mature content notes and is "remarkably easy to miss" (source: https://indiecator.org/2026/06/16/steam-next-fest-has-an-ai-problem-and-players-cant-filter-it-out/; single opinion blog).
- A claim that Valve changed the survey wording silently in May to June 2026 (artland3d, citing Wayback snapshots of 2026-05-13 and 2026-06-17) conflicts with contemporaneous coverage dating the "efficiency gains" wording to 2026-01-16 and later; treat it as unreliable (source: https://artland3d.com/steam-ai-disclosure-rules/, versus the three January sources above).

Named examples: AI used, not disclosed (at the time)

- The Alters (11 bit studios): launched 2025-06-13; players found ChatGPT-style text in an in-game logbook and AI-looking Brazilian Portuguese localization; 80.lv on 2025-06-30 said the Steam page had no AI disclaimer, which it called a violation of Steam's rules (source: https://80.lv/articles/the-alters-developer-gets-criticized-for-not-disclosing-ai-use). The studio's 2025-06-30 statement said AI assets were temporary placeholders, one shipped by accident, AI was used for last-minute translation of licensed movie subtitles (~0.3% of text), and it apologised for not telling players; it did not commit to a Steam disclosure (source: https://gameinformer.com/2025/06/30/11bit-studios-responds-to-the-alters-generative-ai-controversy). A June 2026 opinion piece says no disclosure has appeared on the page (source: https://indiecator.org/2026/06/16/steam-next-fest-has-an-ai-problem-and-players-cant-filter-it-out/; single source, unverified).
- Crimson Desert (Pearl Abyss): players spotted AI-looking 2D props about a day after launch; Pearl Abyss admitted on 2026-03-22 that placeholder assets made with "experimental AI generative tools" were "unintentionally included in the final release" and promised an audit; the Steam page "now" carries a disclosure, i.e. it was added after launch (source: https://engadget.com/gaming/crimson-desert-developer-apologizes-and-promises-to-replace-ai-generated-art-183716439.html). Players asked for refunds citing the late disclosure; Valve refused (ixbt, 2026-03-24, no Valve statement quoted) (source: https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/03/24/valve-otkazalas-vozvrashhat-dengi-za-crimson-desert-igroki-nedovolny-naliciem-ii-assetov-v-igre.html). A search summary of a Notebookcheck article (403 on fetch) says the stated reason was the two-hour playtime limit: unverified (https://www.notebookcheck.net/Crimson-Desert-player-denied-Steam-refund-for-post-release-date-generative-AI-disclosure.1257901.0.html).
- Call of Duty: Black Ops 6 (Activision): launched October 2024; Steam page gained a disclosure reported 2025-02-25 reading "Our team uses generative AI tools to help develop some in-game assets", without saying which (source: https://decrypt.co/307729/call-of-duty-black-ops-6-generative-ai). This is both a late disclosure and a vague one.
- Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 (Sandfall): the Indie Game Awards rescinded two awards in December 2025 after Sandfall confirmed genai use, which it described as placeholder textures removed within days (sources: https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/clair-obscur-expedition-33-game-of-the-year-award-pulled-after-admitting-to-generative-ai-use, headline only; https://stevivor.com/news/clair-obscur-loses-two-indie-game-awards-due-to-ai-usage/, search summary). When fetched on 2026-10-20 the Steam store page showed no AI disclosure section (source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1903340/Clair_Obscur_Expedition_33/; single fetch via summariser). This is arguably compliant under the Jan 2026 rules because the textures did not ship. An indiecator claim that the game launched with a disclosure that was removed days later is single-source: unverified.

Named examples: retroactive and late disclosures, removals

- Haro's Wayback analysis: ~1% of pre-2024 games carry the flag and all of it is retroactive; 22% of significant-revenue AI games added the flag after release (example Crime Scene Cleaner, +20 months); these additions "cluster tightly" around Valve's January 2026 rewrite, which Haro reads as the signature of a compliance sweep (his inference; Valve made no statement); part of the 2026 rise is relabeling of older games (source: https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/three-years-of-ai-on-steam). The 22% is for significant-revenue games only; no full-census figure is given.
- An anecdote on the Steam forums: a user reported a game for non-disclosure and the developer added the disclosure retroactively; no game named (source: https://steamcommunity.com/discussions/forum/0/565912032657840371/).
- Totally Human notes some disclosures belong to old games updated later; example Stellaris, which predates the transformer paper (source: https://totallyhuman.io/blog/games-with-ai-disclosures-have-grossed-an-estimated-660m-on-steam).
- Removal of a disclosure: Jurassic World Evolution 3 (Frontier) replaced AI scientist portraits after backlash on 2025-06-25 and, per Hitmarker, the Steam page no longer shows the AI warning (source: https://hitmarker.net/news/frontier-announces-it-will-remove-ai-artwork-from-jurassic-world-evolution-3-following-player-backlash-1473986); Gamereactor reports the statement but not the disclosure removal (source: https://www.gamereactor.eu/jurassic-world-evolution-3-will-no-longer-use-generative-ai-after-fans-complained-1565003/). Treat the removal as single-source. Indiecator also says some games remove disclosures after replacing AI assets (single source, unverified). Haro's article found no dropped flags in its content, and did not look for them (source: https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/three-years-of-ai-on-steam).
- Messaging drift: a search summary of Eurogamer's Next Fest piece describes a developer whose wording moved from "AI created background assets" to "only used as reference"; original Eurogamer text not retrieved: unverified.

Vague or minimizing wording

- Haro's text analysis (138 successful and 400 flopped AI-flagged games): flops have shorter disclosures (median 13 words vs 22; 35% are one line of 10 words or fewer vs 13%); successful games use more minimizing language (59% vs 41%) and human-oversight reassurance (21% vs 12%), e.g. "reviewed and manually refined", "We do NOT use AI-generated art", and hedges like "Generative AI may have been used" (source: https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/three-years-of-ai-on-steam).
- Totally Human: among $1m+ earners most disclosures describe light, peripheral use with notes such as "no gameplay impact" and "under 1% of content" (source: https://totallyhuman.io/blog/games-with-ai-disclosures-have-grossed-an-estimated-660m-on-steam).
- GamingOnLinux (2025-10-21): wording ranges from specific to a vague "we may use it for background stuff" (source: https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/10/want-to-avoid-ai-gen-on-steam-this-browser-userscript-might-save-your-day/).

Valve forcing a disclosure or delisting

- No report was found of Valve delisting a game already on sale for undisclosed AI, and the conversation piece says Valve could pull titles hit by AI copyright claims but does not say it has (source: https://stuff.co.za/2026/02/12/are-video-game-developers-using-ai-players-want-to-know-but-the-rules-are-patchy/).
- Before the disclosure regime, in 2023, Valve rejected submissions containing AI assets over unclear training-data rights; this is pre-2024 history and seen only through search summaries of techcrunch and nme (https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/03/valve-responds-to-claims-it-has-banned-ai-generated-games-from-steam/): `search-summary`.
- A 2026 claim that Valve now blocks releases that skip AI metadata tagging came from one weak source (tech-insider.org) and has no corroboration: unverified.

### 3. The public debate and the state as of 2026-10-20

- Tim Sweeney, on X (reported by GamingOnLinux 2025-11-27), replying to an unnamed user who wanted Steam to drop "made with AI": "It makes no sense for game stores, where AI will be involved in nearly all future production"; he said the tag is relevant to art exhibits and licensing marketplaces; no Valve response in that article (source: https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/11/according-to-epic-ceo-tim-sweeney-game-stores-dont-need-an-ai-label-as-it-will-be-everywhere/). Techloy repeated it on 2025-12-02, adding Nexon CEO Junghun Lee saying it is "important to assume that every game company is using AI" (source: https://www.techloy.com/epic-ceo-tim-sweeney-says-steam-should-ditch-its-made-with-ai-labels/). The "shampoo brand" jab appears only in a search summary: unverified.
- Sweeney in a PC Gamer interview, relayed by VGC on 2026-06-25: "I think it's really irresponsible of Valve", the disclosure is an AI "Scarlet Letter", a "hater community" tries to kill games; no Valve response included; interview date not stated (source: https://videogameschronicle.com/news/epic-games-ceo-says-its-really-irresponsible-of-steam-to-make-studios-disclose-ai-use).
- Player side: players keep asking for a native filter; as of the June 2026 Next Fest coverage Steam offers none, and SteamDB's tag is the workaround (sources: https://indiecator.org/2026/06/16/steam-next-fest-has-an-ai-problem-and-players-cant-filter-it-out/, https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/02/steamdb-now-lets-you-filter-out-steam-games-with-ai-generation/). A resetera commenter predicts a filter would lead to "no dev disclosing AI use" (speculation) (source: https://www.resetera.com/threads/steam-next-fest-has-over-8-600-demos-and-almost-1700-of-them-have-ai-disclosures.1551832/).
- Commercial effect of the label: Haro finds AI-flagged games reach 100+ reviews at ~55% of the non-AI rate and hold about 10 to 27 percent of estimated sales against ~33% of launches (source: https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/three-years-of-ai-on-steam); a December 2025 "game oracle" analysis reportedly found about half the first-month reviews for disclosing games after controls and is correlational (reported by https://artland3d.com/steam-ai-disclosure-rules/; primary not retrieved: unverified). This is the incentive behind concealment and behind Sweeney's complaint.
- Player perception: an arXiv paper (Bazzaz and Cooper, 2026-08-12, 508,192 English Steam reviews) finds disclosing games get fewer recommendations and more negative sentiment than procedural-generation games, with players reading AI as low developer investment (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11539).
- Valve signals: the only observed movement is the January 2026 narrowing; the disclosure itself stayed. Searches for September and October 2026 changes found nothing, and no Valve statement defending, changing or dropping the requirement was found. State as of 2026-10-20: requirement active, no native filter or tag found in coverage up to June 2026; absence of news proves nothing about changes; re-check the Steamworks Content Survey page before shipping.

### 4. Implications for the extension

Expected false negatives (game uses AI, signal absent)

- AI in dev tools and unshipped placeholder art is exempt by rule (sources in section 2).
- AI shipped but not disclosed, then fixed only after exposure: The Alters, Crimson Desert, Black Ops 6 (about a year late). A point-in-time scrape misses the window before the fix.
- Strategic omission: no incentive to disclose and a stigma ("Scarlet Letter") (Sweeney source in section 3; ResetEra #19 "only the ppl that are willing to disclose, in an environment with no incentive to disclose" is commenter opinion).
- Vague wording that a reader (or a classifier) does not recognise as AI.
- Every count above is a floor (VGC, Totally Human, techraptor all say this).

Expected false positives (flag shown, "uses AI" is misleading)

- Minor or peripheral use: the flag is binary and covers anything from one asset to live generation (source: https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/three-years-of-ai-on-steam).
- Marketing-only or store-page-only AI: the form covers marketing; the flag can mean no in-game AI (source: https://80.lv/articles/steam-has-clarified-its-rules-on-the-use-of-ai-in-video-games).
- Negated or reassuring text in the disclosure ("We do NOT use AI-generated art"); a keyword match on "AI" in the text is unsafe. Key off the section's presence, then show the text (Haro quotes above).
- Stale disclosures after assets were removed (jwe3 shows the page can be updated; whether most developers do it is unknown).
- Older games relabeled in a compliance sweep (Haro) and DLC, demos and software: SteamDB's count covers "games and software" (the conversation).
- SteamDB tag mismatches reported by a reader comment (some undeclared games tagged, some declared games not excluded) are unverified (https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/02/steamdb-now-lets-you-filter-out-steam-games-with-ai-generation/).

Complementary signals

- User reviews that mention AI: usable as a secondary hint; the arXiv study shows AI talk is common and negative, but wrongful accusations and "witch hunts" exist; use it only as a weak "community suspects" cue (sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11539, https://stuff.co.za/2026/02/12/are-video-game-developers-using-ai-players-want-to-know-but-the-rules-are-patchy/).
- SteamDB "AI Content Disclosed" tag: a convenience mirror of the same store-page disclosure and no independent signal (https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/02/steamdb-now-lets-you-filter-out-steam-games-with-ai-generation/). Historical snapshots (Wayback, as Haro used) could detect late and removed disclosures.
- Curator lists and Steam community user tags for AI: not researched or not found in this pass; no confirmation that such lists or tags exist or are maintained: gap.
- Prior art: "AI warning for Steam" (GitHub seeeeew/aiwarningforsteam) is an extension plus userscript that shows the disclosure in a popup on store pages and blurs or hides disclosing games on search pages (sources: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/seeeeew/aiwarningforsteam/main/README.md, https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/10/want-to-avoid-ai-gen-on-steam-this-browser-userscript-might-save-your-day/).
- Design consequences: wording should be "discloses AI use" (never "uses" or "AI-free"); show the developer's own text; absence of a disclosure should render as neutral and never as clean; consider a severity hint (in-game art vs marketing vs live-generated) from the pre/live structure.

## Caveats and version notes

- Policy versions: Jan 2024 original form; Jan 2026 rewrite (form reported on 2026-01-16 by Game Developer, 2026-01-17 by VGC, 2026-01-19 by 80.lv); the 2024 form asked about AI more broadly (gamingbible). Counts before and after the rewrite measure slightly different things.
- Sources differ on denominators (all releases, games only, games and software, event lists) and on snapshot dates; do not chart them as one series.
- The Steam store API `appdetails` response for app 1903340 and for the sample app 5072580 showed no dedicated AI-disclosure field in a single summariser read (the sample's short description and pc requirements mention AI narration and omit the disclosure section); the disclosure appears to live in the store page HTML. This is one read through a summariser: unverified, check directly before relying on it. The sample page (https://store.steampowered.com/app/5072580/Courage_and_Faith_Into_the_Dark_Woods/) was read as opening "Generative AI is used in both pre-generated and live-generated content." with no release date announced; developer Local Fire Games.
- Several outlets (PC Gamer, TechRadar, Notebookcheck, Screenhub, inkl) returned 403 or boilerplate; their content is known only through headlines or search summaries; each such claim is tagged.
- A search result from piefed.keyboardvagabond.com contained an embedded instruction-like string; it was not acted on and that site is not used as a source.
- Sub-questions not fully answered: no Valve statement on counts or on Sweeney; no confirmed Valve delisting for non-disclosure; no verified curator lists or user-tag signals; no first-hand check of the Steamworks page history for post-January-2026 edits.
