# rq1: what does steam's ai content disclosure policy require of developers?

researched 2026-10-20. all fetches were done on that date unless noted. wording of valve text is quoted only in short fragments; everything else is paraphrase.

## answer in short
- valve announced the policy in the steamworks news post "AI Content on Steam" (posted 2024-01-10 00:16 UTC, i.e. 2024-01-09 16:16 US pacific): a new "AI disclosure" section in the steamworks content survey, split into pre-generated and live-generated content. much of the developer's disclosure is shown on the store page, under the heading "AI Generated Content Disclosure".
- the only documented rewrite since is in january 2026 (press coverage 2026-01-16 to 2026-01-20): the survey text now says it is about "content that ships with your game, and is consumed by players" and that efficiency gains from ai dev tools "[are] not the focus". store page, community assets and marketing materials also count. i found no valve blog post for it and no further revision between february and 2026-10-20.
- live-generated content adds three things: describe your guardrails in the survey, a steam overlay path for players to report content that should have been blocked, and a hard ban on adult only sexual content made with live-generated ai ("at this time").
- enforcement is review plus self-report: valve compares survey answers with the build and store page before release and evaluates ai output like any other content, but the public docs state no penalty for lying or omitting, approved games are not re-reviewed on update, and some survey answers need steam support to change after approval. the only stated sanction (app pulled from the store for missing guardrails) comes from one press quote of the login-gated form.

## findings

### 1. what rules did valve introduce, and have they changed since?

**the original announcement (january 2024).** the steamworks development news post is titled "AI Content on Steam", gid 3862463747997849619, group (clan) id 4145017. steam's own event json endpoint gives posttime 1704845796, which is 2024-01-10 00:16:36 UTC (2024-01-09 16:16 pacific) (source: https://store.steampowered.com/events/ajaxgetpartnerevent?clan_accountid=4145017&announcement_gid=3862463747997849619&l=english, fetched twice with consistent values). the same json gives an updatetime of 1705097438 (about 2024-01-12 22:10 UTC), so the body may have been edited three days later; what changed is unverified. press dated it 2024-01-10 (https://www.pcgamesn.com/steam/ai-content-reporting) and 2024-01-11 (https://dashfight.com/news/steam-adds-an-option-to-report-ai-content-4901). the canonical page https://steamcommunity.com/groups/steamworks/announcements/detail/3862463747997849619 returned only its title to my fetcher, so the body text above comes from the json endpoint.

what the 2024 post says, in order: valve said it needed time to study "the fast-moving and legally murky space of AI technology" and was now changing how it handles ai games so it can release "the vast majority of games that use it" (source: ajaxgetpartnerevent url above). the content survey gets "a new AI disclosure section" (same source). developers describe their ai use in two categories, pre-generated and live-generated (definitions in sub-question 2). valve uses the disclosure in pre-release review and will "include much of your disclosure on the Steam store page" (same source). valve also launched a system for players to report illegal content in games with live-generated ai, reachable from the in-game overlay (same source). the one exception to the new openness is adult only sexual content made with live-generated ai (same source).

**the january 2026 rewrite.** this was not announced in a steamworks news post that i could find; it is an edit to the steamworks content survey doc (and, per press, the in-app form). the exact date valve made the edit is unverified. first press coverage and who flagged it:
- game developer, 2026-01-16, "Valve tweaks and clarifies AI disclosure rules for Steam" (source: https://gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-tweaks-and-clarifies-ai-disclosure-rules-for-steam).
- video games chronicle, 2026-01-17, credits simon carless of gamediscoverco, who posted on linkedin (source: https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use). pcgamesn also ran it 2026-01-17 (source: https://www.pcgamesn.com/steam/new-gen-ai-disclosure-form).
- outlook india 2026-01-18 (source: https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/valve-clarifies-steam-ai-policy-focus-shifts-to-content-consumed); dexerto and wnhub 2026-01-19 (sources: https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/steam-makes-major-change-to-ai-rules-for-games-3306274 and https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-49836); gigazine and gamingbible 2026-01-20 (sources: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-steam-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines and https://www.gamingbible.com/news/platform/steam/steam-ai-changes-disclosure-rules-203740-20260120).
- carless's framing, relayed by gigazine: valve "significantly rewrote - but did NOT remove" the form (source: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-steam-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines). treat the disclosure duty as intact.

what the current steamworks doc says (fetched twice on 2026-10-20): section 3 of the content survey, "Generative Artificial Intelligence Content", says the section is not about efficiency gains from ai-powered dev tools and "is concerned with the use of AI in creating content that ships with your game, and is consumed by players", with artwork, sound, narrative and localization as examples (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey). the press quotes of the same sentences match (sources: https://www.pcgamesn.com/steam/new-gen-ai-disclosure-form, https://www.gosugamers.net/entertainment/news/77861-steam-revises-ai-policy-while-keeping-generative-content-disclosure-intact).

what moved between 2024 and 2026:
- scope narrowed from "any kind of content (art/code/sound/etc) created with the help of AI tools during development" (2024 post, ajaxgetpartnerevent url) to content "that ships with your game and is consumed by players" (current doc). the word "code" no longer appears in the pre-generated example list in the doc text i read; whether ai-written code that ships in the game still needs disclosing is unverified (one seo blog claims valve confirmed it still does; i did not open it).
- ai dev tools (code helpers, bug checkers, etc.) are explicitly not the focus (doc, above). examples named in press: github copilot, automated bug-checking software, office productivity tools, and the photoshop generative fill and chatgpt questions raised by pc gamer (source: https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/valve-clarifies-steam-ai-policy-focus-shifts-to-content-consumed). those examples are the press's, not valve's.
- the form now covers ai content for the game, its store page, marketing materials and community assets, in a written explanation, plus a separate gameplay question (sources: https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/steam-makes-major-change-to-ai-rules-for-games-3306274, https://www.pcgamesn.com/steam/new-gen-ai-disclosure-form). kitguru quotes the gameplay question as "Do you use AI to live-generate content or code during gameplay?" (source: https://www.kitguru.net/desktop-pc/mustafa-mahmoud/steam-updates-its-gen-ai-disclosure-policies/). the form itself is behind the steamworks login, so these are second-hand.
- gamingbible says the form went from one broad question to three (source: https://www.gamingbible.com/news/platform/steam/steam-ai-changes-disclosure-rules-203740-20260120). dexerto, game developer and pcgamesn describe two parts. treat "three questions" as unverified.
- pcgamesn adds that the gameplay section now says players can report content they find inappropriate or copyright infringing (a paraphrase, not a direct quote), where the 2024 post said "illegal" (source: https://www.pcgamesn.com/steam/new-gen-ai-disclosure-form). single source.

**changes after january 2026.** none found. targeted searches (standard and extended) for later valve changes, new filters, or new store labels found only january 2026 coverage plus follow-on commentary (the conversation, 2026-02-11, says steam still has no consumer search or filter for ai content; source: https://theconversation.com/are-video-game-developers-using-ai-players-want-to-know-but-the-rules-are-patchy-274850). the current doc carries no date stamps or changelog, so absence of a found change is not proof of none. no 2025 revision found either; the january 2026 coverage calls it the first revision since 2024 (source: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-steam-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines).

**store page wording.** the display heading is "AI Generated Content Disclosure" (named in the vgc january 2026 article, source: https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use, and seen on the sample page, source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/5072580/Courage_and_Faith_Into_the_Dark_Woods/). on that sample page (one fetch, 2026-10-20) the section sits after "About This Game" and the features list and before "Mature Content Description" and "System Requirements". its body is developer-written prose that itself uses the words pre-generated and live-generated (same source). the conversation describes the field as free text, not a tag (source: https://theconversation.com/are-video-game-developers-using-ai-players-want-to-know-but-the-rules-are-patchy-274850). valve's 2024 post only promises "much of" the disclosure, so the shown text is a developer-supplied, valve-selected subset; exactly what valve trims is unverified. i found no change to the heading in 2026.

### 2. pre-generated vs live-generated, and extra obligations for live-generated

**pre-generated.** current doc: content that ships with the game, is consumed by players and was made with ai tools during development (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey). the 2024 wording was "any kind of content (art/code/sound/etc) created with the help of AI tools during development" (source: ajaxgetpartnerevent url above). obligations: under the steam distribution agreement the developer promises the game will not include illegal or infringing content and will be consistent with its marketing materials; valve evaluates the ai output in prerelease review "the same way" as non-ai content (sources: ajaxgetpartnerevent url above; https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey). the full distribution agreement is not public, so its exact clauses are unverified (https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect only mentions it and is access-restricted).

**live-generated.** "any kind of content created with the help of AI tools while the game is running" (both the 2024 post and the current doc; sources above). it follows the same rules as pre-generated plus these:
- guardrails: in the content survey the developer must say what guardrails stop the ai generating illegal content (sources: ajaxgetpartnerevent url above; https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey).
- in-game player reporting: valve built a steam system for players to report illegal content in games with live-generated ai, submitted from the in-game overlay when they meet content that guardrails should have caught (source: ajaxgetpartnerevent url above). this is a valve-side system, not something the developer builds; outlook india wording ("developers must support a Steam overlay") is a loose paraphrase (source: https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/valve-clarifies-steam-ai-policy-focus-shifts-to-content-consumed). the current steamworks doc page does not mention the overlay or reporting at all (my keyword check found no "overlay" in body text). valve has not said publicly what it does with reports (source: https://www.pcgamesn.com/steam/ai-content-reporting).
- adults-only restriction: adult only sexual content made with live-generated ai is not accepted. 2024: "we are unable to release that type of content right now" and valve would "revisit this decision when necessary" (ajaxgetpartnerevent url above). current doc faq: valve does not want to ship it "at this time", citing substantial legal and customer risk (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey). pre-generated adult content is not covered by this ban.
- paid ai services: if the game calls a paid live-generated ai service with per-call cost, the developer must manage player access and take payment through a steam-supported method: price it into the game, microtransactions, a subscription, or a single dlc (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey). when this faq entry was added is unverified.
- stated sanction for guardrails (single source): pcgamesn quotes the in-app form as saying it is the developer's responsibility to implement guard rails so users are not exposed to inappropriate and illegal content, and "Failure to do so will result in your app being pulled from the store" (source: https://www.pcgamesn.com/steam/new-gen-ai-disclosure-form). i could not confirm this in the public steamworks doc (no match for "pulled from the store") or in a second independent outlet; letsdatascience repeats a softer version that looks derived (source: https://letsdatascience.com/news/valve-updates-steam-ai-disclosure-for-player-consumed-conten-15e5014d). tag as unverified beyond pcgamesn.

### 3. how it is enforced

**survey and review.** every app must complete the content survey (general content, mature content, generative ai content) before the store page and build are submitted for review; after submission valve's team "will compare the answers relative to the content in your build and on your store page" (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey). the review pipeline doc (store page, build, adult content, trading cards) mentions no ai check of its own and states no penalties (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/review_process). survey answers do not excuse content: products "must adhere to the content rules, regardless of whether it is disclosed in these surveys" (doc faq, same source as the survey).

**lying or failing to disclose.** the public steamworks docs state no specific penalty for a false or missing ai disclosure (checked: content survey, review process, releasing docs; sources: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey, https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/review_process, https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/releasing). the survey's mature-content section asks for "honest and accurate disclosures" (same survey doc), which is the nearest wording to an accuracy duty and is not specific to the ai section. the one stated sanction is the guardrail line above (pcgamesn only). the conversation says it is unclear what happens when a developer fails to disclose (source: https://theconversation.com/are-video-game-developers-using-ai-players-want-to-know-but-the-rules-are-patchy-274850).

observed cases (all secondary):
- call of duty / black ops 6: a steam note "Our team uses generative AI tools to help develop some in-game assets" appeared on 2025-01-27 per steamdb, about a month after suspected ai content shipped; no removal and no valve comment reported (source: https://hitmarker.net/news/activision-confirms-call-of-duty-features-ai-generated-content-via-steam-1275002).
- crimson desert (march 2026): pearl abyss added a generative ai note after launch when ai art was spotted; a reddit user who refunded after about 4 hours was declined on playtime grounds; no special valve action reported (sources, search-result summaries only because the pages returned 403: https://www.notebookcheck.net/Crimson-Desert-player-denied-Steam-refund-for-post-release-date-generative-AI-disclosure.1257901.0.html, https://www.techspot.com/news/111795-crimson-desert-dev-apologizes-ai-art-slop-promises.html).
- the alters (11 bit studios): apologised for undisclosed ai assets; no removal reported (search-result summary only, hitmarker).
- a techraptor analysis relayed by a newsletter says 18% of steam's top 100 new 2025 releases used generative ai and fewer than half of those told players on the store page (source: https://pixelpnl.beehiiv.com/p/pixel-p-l-nearly-one-in-five-top-steam-games-now-use-ai). i did not see the original; unverified.
- i found no report of valve delisting a game solely for failing to disclose ai. the 2023 heard of the story removal predates the policy and was about an ai feature, not a disclosure (search-result summary only).

**editing disclosures later.** the doc faq says that once the build and store page are approved, some survey questions "cannot be edited without first contacting" steam support, who want a summary of the change, which survey result it affects, and how a tester can reach the content (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey). which questions are locked, and whether the ai questions are, is not stated: unverified. the store page text clearly can be updated after launch, as call of duty and crimson desert show (sources above). approved games are not re-reviewed: "Once your game has been reviewed and approved, there is no need to go through review again" (source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/review_process, fetched twice). inference, not stated by valve: ai content added in a later build is not necessarily seen by review, so the shipped game can drift from the disclosure.

**is it purely self-reported?** mostly. valve's stated checks are the pre-release comparison of survey, build and store page (doc, above) and player reports for live-generated content (2024 post). press characterises the policy as voluntary and not enforced by moderation (gigazine's summary of gamespot, https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-steam-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines; the gamespot article itself was only seen as a search-result summary, https://www.gamespot.com/articles/valve-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines-to-allow-for-ai-powered-tools/1100-6537483/). "voluntary" is the press's word; valve's doc makes the survey mandatory but does not describe independent verification. community trackers exist because of the gap: steamdb's "AI Content Disclosed" tag (12,284 games, 2025-10-21, https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/10/want-to-avoid-ai-gen-on-steam-this-browser-userscript-might-save-your-day/; more than 15,000 per the conversation, 2026-02-11) and the user-run "AI Check" curator group (the conversation).

## caveats and version notes
- the steamworks doc has no changelog; "current" means the page as fetched on 2026-10-20. old wording comes from the 2024 post, not from an archived copy of the doc (web.archive.org was blocked to my fetcher).
- the in-app survey form (where the guardrail sanction and exact question text live) is behind the steamworks login; all form text above is relayed by press and is not independently verified, except the sentences that also appear in the public doc.
- the json for the 2024 post includes an rtime32_last_modified of 1792301324 (about 2026-10-18), returned identically in two fetches. i could not explain it; it may be a platform field, and i did not treat it as evidence of an edit.
- many search results are seo blogs (tech-insider, legalmoveslawfirm, strayspark and similar); none were used as a source for a claim. one search result contained text that looked like an embedded instruction; it was ignored.
- these pages returned 403 or truncated content to my fetcher, so claims about them rest on search-result summaries: geekwire, techpowerup, bluesnews, notebookcheck, techspot, and the pc gamer january 2026 article (headline only).
- sample game: Courage and Faith: Into the Dark Woods (app 5072580), developer and publisher Local Fire Games, release "coming soon" at fetch time; its disclosure says generative ai is used in both pre-generated and live-generated content (source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/5072580/Courage_and_Faith_Into_the_Dark_Woods/). one fetch only.
- incidental, outside rq1 but relevant to the project: prior art exists. "AI warning for Steam" (repo seeeeew/aiwarningforsteam, file aiwarningforsteam.user.js) is a firefox/chrome extension and userscript that shows the disclosure in a modal on store pages and blurs or hides disclosed games in search (source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/seeeeew/aiwarningforsteam/main/README.md). its readme does not say how it detects disclosures. gamespot covered it (title only seen in search results).
- implications for the extension: the store signal only means the developer chose to disclose; dev-tool ai is exempt by design; the section text is free-form so "pre-generated" and "live-generated" can only be inferred from developer wording, not read from a structured field (unverified whether the live-generated checkbox is rendered distinctly on the store page).
