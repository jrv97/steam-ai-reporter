## Signals that stay quiet

- No user complained about speed or memory in any tool, including ones that fetch a full page per row. The cost is invisible to users until Steam answers with a 429. The ceeprus author reports about 120 requests per page and 429 responses (rq8 s6). Efficiency is a rule-13 requirement for us, and the data gives no market push behind it.
- No tool reads the batched GetItems endpoint, so the market has no break record for it. Every recorded break concerns heading text or page markup.
- No outside party has reviewed the accuracy of any tool. No tool publishes a false-positive or miss rate.

## Open questions this note cannot answer

- Whether the Chrome-store audience of the leader differs from its Firefox audience. The Chrome text reviews skew lower (5 of 13 at three stars or fewer, tracker) while all 32 Firefox ratings are five stars (rq2 s8).
- How many users want undisclosed-use data enough to accept a server. The demand signal is real, and the price is a crowd list that breaks our privacy rule.
- Whether demo apps carry the disclosure in the API. Slop-Inator inherits the parent app's flag for DLC, demo and soundtrack pages (rq7 s2). The reason is not recorded. Next Fest tiles are demos.
