# Store listing copy

Source text for the Chrome Web Store and addons.mozilla.org (AMO) listings of "Tag Kit for Steam" (short name "Tag Kit"). The listing text is English; the extension also ships a Spanish catalog (name "Tag Kit para Steam", short name "Tag Kit"). The userscript has no store listing; its text is the `@description` in the userscript header, which comes from the same message catalog as the manifests. Its `@name` stays "AI Reporter for Steam" for now; see [Renaming the userscript](../testing/userscript-install.mdx#renaming-the-userscript).

This copy describes the release that includes pull requests 85 to 91: the Family Sharing tag, the tag order, the chip text and colors, and the settings tabs. All of them are merged on main as of 2026-10-27, and no earlier release matches it.

Wording follows the project's semantics rule: the AI chip says "AI" and the card gives the developer's own words. Nothing here says a game "uses AI" or is "AI-free", because developers report their AI use themselves.

## Short description for AMO (250 characters at most; this text is 244)

Tags Steam items with what Steam and developers report: AI Generated Content Disclosure, anti-cheat, 3rd-party DRM, account and EULA, PvP, In-App Purchases, Steam Deck, Steam Machine and Family Sharing. Show, dim or hide them. Collects nothing.

## Detailed description

Tag Kit for Steam tags items on the Steam store with what Steam and the developers report: AI Generated Content Disclosure, Kernel Level Anti-Cheat and Anti-Cheat Software, 3rd-party DRM, 3rd-Party Account, 3rd-party EULA, PvP, In-App Purchases, Family Sharing, and Valve's Steam Deck and Steam Machine ratings.

It reads Steam's own store data. It sends no reports, collects nothing and has no server of its own.

What it does:

- Puts a short chip on capsules and rows in search, wishlist, home, tag and category hubs, sale pages, developer pages, package pages and search suggestions. Hovering a chip, or focusing it with the keyboard, opens a card with each tag in Steam's own words and its source.
- Adds a section "Tag Kit tags for this product" on a game's page, and a banner that quotes the developer's AI disclosure word for word.
- Lets you set each tag on its own: on or off, the badge, its entry in the banner and a list filter (Show, Dim or Hide).
- Counts the results it hid in search and lets you reveal them on that page.
- Lets you ignore a game, a developer or a publisher, for one tag or for all tags. Ignoring a game also covers its DLC.
- Lets you reorder the tags by drag and drop, word your own chip text and pick badge colors.
- Exports and imports your settings.
- Comes in English and Spanish.

What each tag means, in Steam's words:

- AI Generated Content Disclosure: the developer reported generative AI use. The chip reads "AI".
- Kernel Level Anti-Cheat and Anti-Cheat Software: the game lists that anti-cheat.
- 3rd-party DRM, 3rd-Party Account and 3rd-party EULA: the game lists that requirement.
- PvP and In-App Purchases: Steam lists them among the game's features. Both tags start switched off.
- Steam Deck and Steam Machine: Valve rated the game Verified, Playable or Unsupported on that device. Both tags start switched off, and you pick which ratings to tag, Unsupported at first. A game Valve has not rated gets no tag.
- Family Sharing: Steam lists Family Sharing among the game's features, which Valve computes. For a paid game whose list leaves it out, the chip reads "No Family Sharing" and the card says "According to the features Steam lists for this game". Free games and non-game items show nothing. This tag starts switched off, and you pick which of the two to tag, "No Family Sharing" at first.

The AI tag is self-reported and says nothing about how much AI a game uses. A game without a chip may still involve AI: developers may not have reported anything, and Steam may not have the information for every item.

Privacy: nothing is collected. To check the items on the page you are viewing, their app IDs go to Valve's own hosts that your browser uses (api.steampowered.com and store.steampowered.com), without cookies. Results and your settings stay in your browser. The full policy is at https://github.com/jrv97/steam-ai-reporter/blob/main/PRIVACY.md

## Single-purpose statement (Chrome Web Store)

Tag Kit for Steam has one purpose: to show which Steam store items carry tags for AI disclosure, anti-cheat, DRM, account, EULA, PvP, in-app purchases, Steam Deck and Steam Machine ratings, and Family Sharing, by marking those items.

## Permission justifications

The extension asks for one permission and no host permissions. It has no background script.

- `storage`: keeps the cache of results (an app is not requested again until its entry expires), your settings (including chip text, colors and tag order), your lists of ignored games, developers and publishers, the settings tab you last opened, and a small record used to back off if Steam is limiting requests. All of it stays on your device in the extension's local storage.
- Site access (content script matches `https://store.steampowered.com/*`): the content script runs only on the Steam store, because that is where the items to mark are. Its requests to `api.steampowered.com` and `store.steampowered.com` are made from the store page's own origin, so no `host_permissions` entry is needed. Both are Valve hosts the user is already on, and every request is made with `credentials: "omit"`.
- Remote code: none. No `eval`, no remote scripts and no remote selectors. The bundles in the package are not minified and are built from the public source.

## Privacy answers

These match PRIVACY.md.

- Does it collect or transmit user data to the developer or any third party? No. There is no analytics, no telemetry, no account and no server of the developer.
- Does it send any data off the device? Only app IDs of the items on the page being viewed, to Valve's own hosts `api.steampowered.com` (GetItems, in batches of up to 50) and `store.steampowered.com` (a few app pages as a fallback), without cookies. Valve sees the IP address and the app IDs, as for any store request.
- What is stored locally? Results per app ID (which tags the store data gives the item, check time, the developer's AI report text and language, the name of a named AI service if any, the game's name), the settings, the ignore lists, the last settings tab and a back-off record. In `storage.local`.
- Does it read the Steam account, wishlist contents, library, cookies or browsing history? No.
- Is data sold or used for purposes unrelated to the single purpose? No.
- Chrome Web Store data usage categories: none are collected. Certify the three limited-use statements (not sold, not used for unrelated purposes, not used for creditworthiness or lending).
- AMO `data_collection_permissions`: `required: ["none"]`, set in the Gecko manifest overlay (`manifest.gecko.json`).
- Privacy policy URL: https://github.com/jrv97/steam-ai-reporter/blob/main/PRIVACY.md

## AMO reviewer notes

The package is built from source, unminified. Build steps are in the README under "Build from source", and the source archive is `steam-ai-reporter-source-<version>.zip`:

1. Install mise (`curl https://mise.run | sh`) on Linux or macOS, or see the mise install guide for Windows.
2. In the unpacked source folder run `mise trust`, then `mise install`, then `mise run build`.
3. `dist/gecko` is the submitted add-on.

The extension has no background script, requests no host permissions and makes no network request other than the two Valve hosts above.
