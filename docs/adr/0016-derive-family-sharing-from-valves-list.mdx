# Derive a value from a category Valve computes when its own list leaves it out

## Context and problem statement

Steam lists Family Sharing among a game's features as category 62 (`category_family_sharing`) in `categories.feature_categoryids`. Unlike the categories the developer sets in Steamworks (In-App Purchases, the PvP modes), category 62 is computed by Valve (`computed: 1` in Steam's category table), and every released item GetItems answers for carries a feature list. Steam has no field that says a game cannot be shared: the client's "Family Sharing not Available" belongs to a signed-in owner's library, and the store's own exclusion reasons ("Free games cannot be shared", "This DLC is not available for Family Sharing", "This type of content cannot be shared") are per license.

The warning use case is the absence: a person who shares a library wants to see which games will not reach the family. Rule 4 as written says an item with `extra_details` and no key for a kind is not reported, which rules that out. [ADR 0015](0015-kinds-valve-computes.mdx) named Valve as a source and left the rule's text for this case: "The wording that names a kind's source and a value derived from a field Steam computes for every item belongs with the Family Sharing slice."

May a kind read Valve's own computed list and tag a game whose list leaves its category out, and under which conditions and in which words? This ADR amends rule 4 and the statement of [ADR 0010](0010-new-tags-from-getitems.mdx) that "an absent key stays 'not reported'", for kinds Valve computes only.

## Decision drivers

- Rule 4: three states; unknown is never shown as not disclosed; no negative of our own ("none", "no X", "DRM-free", "no anti-cheat"); Steam's words.
- Principle 1: every fact is attributed to whoever states it.
- Rule 1: Valve hosts only, no credentials, no seed lists. The client's per-owner status needs a signed-in request.
- No false flags: a free game, a DLC whose base game decides, a demo or an unreleased game must not read as "not available" because its list lacks 62.
- Rule 13: no new request and no section flag; the categories come with every item.

## Considered options

1. No Family Sharing tag.
2. A positive-only tag: "Family Sharing" when the list holds 62, nothing else.
3. A positive tag and a derived one: "Family Sharing" when the list holds 62, and Steam's "Family Sharing not Available" when Valve's non-empty list for a paid game leaves 62 out (chosen).
4. The negative from the signed-in client (`FamilyGroup_GameExcluded`, the owner's library).

## Decision outcome

Chosen option 3, under these conditions:

- The kind reads Valve's computed category list only (`source: "valve"`); no developer-declared kind derives anything from absence, and rule 4 keeps "not reported" for them.
- Supported, the facet `supported`: the feature list holds 62.
- Derived, the facet `unsupported`: the feature list is non-empty, it lacks 62, the item is a game (`type` 0) and it is not free (`is_free` is not true). Steam's JSON leaves `is_free` out for paid games and sends `true` for free ones (every capture on hand): an absent field reads as paid and a field that is not a boolean makes the kind unknown `malformed`.
- Not disclosed: a non-empty list without 62 on a free game or on any other item type (a DLC, a demo, a package). Steam says free games cannot be shared, which would put the tag on every free-to-play game, and a DLC follows its base game (ARC Raiders lists 62 and its cosmetic DLC 5201760 and 4210150 do not). Not disclosed renders nothing and keeps the cache life of a known answer, a week, where an unknown answer would refetch the item every 6 hours.
- Unknown: the categories malformed (`malformed`), and the feature list absent or empty (`missing`); a demo carries none, and an empty list says no more than an absent one, since Steam's JSON leaves an empty list out.
- Words: the heading "Family Sharing" and the value words "Family Sharing" and "Family Sharing not Available" are Steam's (`AppDetails_Feature_FamilySharing`, `DisplayStatus_Compact_OwnerLocked`; Spanish "Prestamo familiar" and "Prestamo familiar no disponible", with the accents the catalog keeps). The hover card closes with "According to Valve", and under the derived value only our own line says what the data shows and nothing more: "Family Sharing is not in the features Steam lists for this game." Nothing renders as "No Family Sharing", "none" or a claim that the game cannot be shared.

Rule 4 in `AGENTS.md` changes from:

> 4. **Semantics.** Three verdict states: disclosed, not disclosed, unknown. Unknown is never stored or shown as not disclosed. An item without `extra_details` is unknown; an item with `extra_details` and no key for a kind is not reported. Neither state renders as "none", "DRM-free" or "no anti-cheat". A tag uses Steam's own words for its kind, and our own copy never says "uses AI", "AI-free" or "no AI". The developer's own text is rendered verbatim through `textContent`.

to:

> 4. **Semantics.** Three verdict states: disclosed, not disclosed, unknown. Unknown is never stored or shown as not disclosed. An item without `extra_details`, or without a section a kind reads, is unknown; an item with them and no key for a kind is not reported. Only a kind read from Valve's own computed category list may derive a value from a non-empty list that lacks its category, under the conditions of an ADR (ADR 0016). Neither state nor a derived value renders as "none", "no" and the kind, "DRM-free" or "no anti-cheat". A tag uses Steam's own words for its kind and names its source: the developer, or Valve for what Valve computes. Our own copy never says "uses AI", "AI-free" or "no AI", and states nothing about a game beyond Steam's words and data. The developer's own text is rendered verbatim through `textContent`.

A catalog check fails on a message that starts with "No " and a tag's heading, or that says "free of" or "none" about a tag, which keeps the wording rule mechanical.

### Library check (rule 12)

Nothing to adopt: the kind reads one integer in a list and two item fields, and the facets, the source line and the settings are the ones of ADR 0015 and decision 61.

### Consequences

- Good: a person who shares a library sees which paid games Steam does not list for Family Sharing, in Steam's words, with Valve named as the source.
- Good: no new request, flag or permission; the categories already come with every item.
- Good: free games, DLC, demos and packages never get the derived value.
- Bad: the derived value rests on Valve's list being complete for released paid games. A game Valve lists late (an unreleased game can list 62 before release, ReBlade 3489340 does) would read as not available until the list changes; the kind's week TTL bounds how long that lasts.
- Bad: the store's exclusions are per license, and a game can be listed and still be excluded for one owner; the tag says what the list shows, not what one library allows.
- Neutral: the canary checks the structure Valve sends (a feature list on every sampled game, 62 on some, a free game without it) and pins no game to a result.

## Pros and cons of the options

### 1. No Family Sharing tag

- Good: nothing to explain.
- Bad: the question people ask before buying a game for a family library goes unanswered, though Valve publishes the data.

### 2. Positive-only tag

- Good: no derived state; rule 4 unchanged.
- Bad: most paid games list 62, so the tag marks nearly every row and the warning use case, the games that will not be shared, is not served.

### 3. Positive and derived tag

- Good: serves the warning use case from data Valve computes for every item, in Steam's words.
- Bad: a rule 4 amendment and a condition list that must stay narrow.

### 4. The signed-in client's status

- Good: exact for one owner's library.
- Bad: needs the person's credentials and the client's endpoints, against rule 1, and says nothing about games the person does not own.

## Amendment 2026-10-26: the maintainer's wording for the derived value

Amended 2026-10-26 by the maintainer. With the chips in Steam's full words, the maintainer chose wording of our own for the derived value, knowing that it departs from rule 4 as written above and from the catalog check this ADR added.

### Decision

- The derived value's chip reads "No Family Sharing" (Spanish "Sin prestamo familiar", with the accent the catalog keeps). The listed value's chip stays Steam's "Family Sharing".
- In the hover card, the derived value's line reads "Family Sharing is not supported for this game" (Spanish "El prestamo familiar no esta disponible para este juego", with the accents the catalog keeps; our own wording, not checked against Steam's). It replaces Steam's client words "Family Sharing not Available" as the card's value line and our earlier line "Family Sharing is not in the features Steam lists for this game.", which is gone.
- The card's source line for this kind reads "According to the features Steam lists for this game" (Spanish "Segun las caracteristicas que Steam indica para este juego", with the accents the catalog keeps, unverified) instead of "According to Valve". A kind names its own line in `KindMessages.source`, `badge_card_valve` when absent, and Steam Deck and Steam Machine keep "According to Valve". A card with entries of several kinds lists each distinct line once, the developer's first.
- Steam's "Family Sharing not Available" stays where no source line follows it: the banner heading, the settings choice, the package summary and the chip's accessible name.
- Scope: such wording is allowed only for a kind read from Valve's computed category list under the conditions above (a non-empty list that lacks the category, a game, not free), and only Family Sharing uses it. Developer-declared kinds keep rule 4 as written: "no AI", "AI-free", "no anti-cheat", "DRM-free" and "none" stay banned for them and for every other kind.
- The catalog check allows the two keys `kind_family_unsupported_chip` and `kind_family_unsupported_line`, by key and not by pattern, and still fails on "No" and a tag's name under every other key.

Rule 4 in `AGENTS.md` changes from the text quoted above, as this ADR first set it, to:

> 4. **Semantics.** Three verdict states: disclosed, not disclosed, unknown. Unknown is never stored or shown as not disclosed. An item without `extra_details`, or without a section a kind reads, is unknown; an item with them and no key for a kind is not reported. Only a kind read from Valve's own computed category list may derive a value from a non-empty list that lacks its category, under the conditions of an ADR (ADR 0016). Neither state nor a derived value renders as "none", "no" and the kind, "DRM-free" or "no anti-cheat", with one exception (ADR 0016, amended): Family Sharing's derived value may read "No Family Sharing" on its chip and "Family Sharing is not supported for this game" in the hover card, which then always closes with "According to the features Steam lists for this game". No other kind, and no developer-declared kind, has such an exception. A tag uses Steam's own words for its kind and names its source: the developer, or Valve for what Valve computes. Our own copy never says "uses AI", "AI-free" or "no AI", and states nothing about a game beyond Steam's words and data, apart from that qualified exception. The developer's own text is rendered verbatim through `textContent`.

### Considered options

1. Keep Steam's words: "Family Sharing not Available" on the chip and as the card's value line, with our line about Steam's list under it.
2. A short form without "no", such as the first build's "Family not Available".
3. "No Family Sharing" on the chip and "Family Sharing is not supported for this game" in the card, qualified by a source line that names Steam's feature list (chosen).

- Option 1. Good: Steam's own words, and rule 4 unchanged. Bad: a status from the signed-in client's library, not from the store, and long on a chip (28 characters, 31 in Spanish).
- Option 2. Good: no negative of our own. Bad: it shortens Steam's name, which the maintainer rejected for the chips, and still reads as the client's status.
- Option 3. Good: short and plain (17 characters, 21 in Spanish), and it reads as the warning a person who shares a library looks for. Bad: our own negative, which rule 4 banned, and an allow-list in the catalog check.

### Consequences

- The card now states the fact in our own words, qualified by the source line that names the features Steam lists for the game.
- The chip alone does not carry the qualifier: its hover card and its accessible name do. The chip's accessible name stays Steam's "Family Sharing not Available", which holds Steam's heading and the value, and does not start with the visible "No Family Sharing", as with the device ratings' chips.
- A demo's lent value shows "For the full game", the line and the kind's own source line.
- The rest of this ADR stands: the conditions, the states, the evidence and the canary are unchanged.
