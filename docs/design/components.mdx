# Tag Kit for Steam UI components (v1)

Component specs for everything the extension and the userscript inject into store pages or own outright. This folder mirrors the published design system (https://claude.ai/artifact/3xsXmaA5vjofhEWkL8nYsQ, private to the account that published it): change one, then sync the other. The UI step of `docs/plans/steam-ai-reporter-v1.md` (step 10) builds from this file, and the i18n step (step 4) authors `src/locales/en/messages.json` from the message catalog at the end.

**Files**

- `docs/design/tokens.css`: every `--sar-*` custom property. The only source of colors, sizes and fonts.
- `docs/design/reference.css`: reference rules for each component below, written against the tokens only. The UI step can lift them into `src/core/ui/styles.css` as they are.
- `docs/design/previews/`: one page per component, the same previews the design system shows, rendering the reference rules on sampled store grounds, synthetic cover art and long Spanish copy.
- `docs/design/specimen.html`: every preview on one page. Open it from disk to review a change.
- `assets/icons/`: the extension icon (section 14).
- `docs/design/decisions.mdx`: why each choice was made, and what is still open.

Tag Kit for Steam tags games on the Steam store whose developers declared something players weigh before buying: AI use, kernel-level or other anti-cheat, third-party DRM, a required third-party account or a third-party EULA. It also tags games with a PvP mode or In-App Purchases, from Steam's own categories, with Valve's own Steam Deck and Steam Machine ratings, and with Family Sharing as the features Valve computes list it, for the values a person picks. Each tag is written in Steam's words, and the developer's text is quoted as written. It ships as a browser extension and a userscript. Its UI lives in two places: small pieces injected into store pages (chips, a banner, a hover card, a list filter) and its own settings pages (options page, toolbar popup, userscript dialog). This system covers both.

Every class, attribute and custom property starts with `sar-`.

## Principles

1. **Label the report, never judge it.** A report can cover one marketing image or live generated gameplay, and some notes say AI was barely used. Attribute every fact to the developer ("reported", "named by the developer", "in the developer's own words"). No red, no warning triangles. Our own colors carry no verdict. A person's custom tag color (experimental, decision 49) is their pick for that tag, so it changes how a tag looks and never what it says.
2. **Nothing for absence.** "Not disclosed" and "unknown" render nothing: no neutral chip, no check mark. The host's `data-sar-kinds` leaves them out.
3. **Quote verbatim.** The developer's notes are data: set with `textContent` and `white-space: pre-line`, never translated, summarized or highlighted.
4. **Feel native.** Look and behave as close to the store's own UI as possible: its sizes, type, neutrals, surfaces and interaction patterns, so injected UI has no seam. Model each store-page component on its closest store element (the banner on the notice bubbles, the full badge on the tag chips, the compact badge on the corner flags, the tag section on the store's tag row). Only the violet hue marks a component as ours, so nobody mistakes it for a store feature. A person can swap the violet for their own color per tag (experimental, decision 49); the defaults stay violet.
5. **Never block.** No modals over store pages, no overlays over content, no focus stealing, no layout shift after a component's first paint.
6. **Copy lives in catalogs.** Components hold message keys, never text. Leave room for translations about 40% longer than English.
7. **Cheap at scale.** Express state with `data-sar-*` attributes and CSS, never per-node inline styles. A setting change is one attribute write per kind on the root element, and a custom color is one custom property per kind on the root element. The one exception is the values a tag with facets picks: a change to them draws the hosts again, writing only what changed (decision 61).

## Voice and copy

- Use Steam's words for each tag: its own line for the kind ("Uses Kernel Level Anti-Cheat", "Incorporates 3rd-party DRM", "Requires 3rd-Party Account", "Requires agreement to a 3rd-party EULA", "PvP", "In-App Purchases", "Steam Deck Compatibility", "Steam Machine Compatibility", "Family Sharing") and, for AI, Steam's heading "AI Generated Content Disclosure". A rating keeps Steam's word for its value: Verified, Playable, Unsupported, and Family Sharing keeps Steam's "Family Sharing" and "Family Sharing not Available" (decision 64). Valve's Unknown (0) is never shown, and never as Unsupported. Spanish follows Steam's Spain Spanish. Our own copy never says "uses AI", "AI-free" or "no AI", and never states a tag's absence ("No" and a tag's name, or "none", "free of" or "-free" beside one), in any locale; the copy test fails the build on them.
- Plain, short, sentence case: "Ignore this game", "Ignore for this tag", "Show full report".
- One line names a tag everywhere: Steam's line is the hover card entry title, the banner entry heading, the settings section title and the start of the chip's accessible name. The chip itself is short: AI, KLAC, Anti-Cheat, DRM, Account, EULA, PvP, IAP. A tag with values puts the value after a short device word: "Deck Unsupported", "Steam Machine Playable" (`kind_facet_chip`), and its accessible name and banner heading read "Steam Deck Compatibility: Unsupported" (`kind_facet_name`). A value with a chip of its own (`FacetDefinition.chip`) shows that chip, which for Family Sharing is Steam's full word, never an abbreviation ("Family Sharing" or "Family Sharing not Available"; the chip reuses `kind_family_heading` and `kind_family_unsupported`), and is named by Steam's word alone. On a narrow compact badge the label may end in an ellipsis (`.sar-compact-label`). Chip text a person can customize is a later backlog item.
- Name the source: the developer ("In the developer's own words:", "AI service named by the developer:", "Declared by the developer on the store page"), or Valve for what Valve computes, the device ratings and Family Sharing ("According to Valve"; decisions 63 and 64). Under Family Sharing not Available only, our line `kind_family_unlisted` says what Steam's list shows and nothing about the game.
- The opt-out labels are "Ignore for this tag" and "Ignore this game" (every tag). The store has its own Ignore button just above; ours only affects this extension.
- Settings speak about games, not switches: the list filter reads "Show", "Dim", "Hide".
- No exclamation marks, no emoji, no em dashes.
- The full catalog, with translator notes, is in the Messages section.

## Color

One dark theme. Store pages are dark, and the settings pages open from them.

- **Neutrals are sampled.** `sar-color-bg-page`, `sar-color-bg-page-deep` and `sar-color-bg-row` are the store's own grounds: our components sit on them and never paint them. `sar-color-bg-panel`, `sar-color-bg-raised` and `sar-color-bg-sunken` are the settings pages' surfaces.
- **Text:** on the banner wash, everything is `sar-banner-fg`, the store's own bubble text color. Elsewhere, `sar-color-text-strong` for headings, `sar-color-text` for body copy, `sar-color-text-muted` for meta lines, `sar-color-text-subtle` for help and dates. Muted and subtle never go on the banner wash, and subtle never on the store's row hover.
- **Violet is ours.** Every other hue already means something on the store: blue is links, tags and the wishlist; green is buy, discount and owned; tan is the store's own third-party notices; red is live. Violet carries no traffic-light verdict, which matters because the badge must not judge. It stays the default for every tag. A person may give a tag a color of their own (see "A tag's own color" below); that is theirs to choose and lifts the guarantees of this page for them alone.
- **The accent scale:** `sar-accent-100` for compact badge text, `sar-accent-200` for full badge text, `sar-accent-300` for links and the focus ring, `sar-accent-400` for non-text brand fills and edges (and the full badge tint at 20%), `sar-accent-800` and `sar-accent-600` at 80% for the banner wash, `sar-accent-600` and `sar-accent-700` for solid fills with white text, `sar-accent-900` for the compact badge's dark surface.
- **A tag's own color (experimental).** One hex color for each tag, entered on the full options page and in the userscript dialog (section 6), not in the toolbar popup. It plays the role of accent 400 and every other shade is mixed from it at a fixed share (`sar-kind-mix-*` in `tokens.css`): the full badge tint and the chips of the Tag Kit section (the color at 20%), their text and the hover card title (the color mixed 37% into white), the compact badge's surface (22% into black), its text (24% into white) and its edge (the color), and the banner wash (31% and 66% into black, both drawn at 80%). Switches, buttons, focus rings, the banner's text and every neutral keep their tokens. The color is used as given: nothing clamps, adjusts or checks its contrast, so a very light or very dark pick can fall below the AA and 3:1 pairs of the Contrast section for the person who picked it. Unset keeps today's violet and its guarantees. A banner with one tag takes that tag's color for the wash; a banner that lists several tags keeps the default violet. Forced colors still override it.
- **Error** is text, always with words, never a red fill: `sar-color-error` on extension pages, `sar-banner-error` inside the banner.
- Never set a store hue (blue, green, tan, red) to a new meaning.

Every text pair meets WCAG 2.2 AA on the grounds its token's note names; see the Contrast section.

## Type

- **Store pages:** the `page` family, which names the store's own "Motiva Sans" first. It is referenced by name only, never bundled; outside the store it falls back to Noto Sans or Arial.
- **The summary box, the tag section and the hidden results notice:** the `summary` family (Arial), like the store's own summary rows, tag chips and search notice bar.
- **Extension pages:** the `ext` family, a system UI stack. The store font is not available there.
- Sizes follow the store's 11, 12, 13, 15, 18 and 24px steps, plus 14/21 for quoted notes, the size the store sets its own disclosure section in. The banner's title and meta lines are set light (300), like the store's own notices, and its notes italic, like the store's disclosure section. Use the named styles: `sar-text-badge`, `sar-text-compact`, `sar-text-compact-lg`, `sar-text-banner-title`, `sar-text-banner-meta`, `sar-text-banner-quote`, `sar-text-banner-button`, `sar-text-banner-note` on store pages; `sar-text-options-title`, `sar-text-card-title`, `sar-text-popup-caption`, `sar-text-body`, `sar-text-help` on extension pages.
- Never shrink type to fit a translation; truncate a badge or let the block wrap.

## Space, radius, layering

- Spacing is the store's React scale: `sar-space-0` to `sar-space-6` (2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32px). Badges keep their own exact paddings (`sar-badge-pad-inline`, `sar-compact-pad-inline`).
- Radii are small or none, like the store's: none on the banner and the compact badge (like the notice bubbles and corner flags), `sar-radius-xs` (1px) on the allow-listed note, `sar-radius-sm` (2px) for the full badge, buttons and segments, `sar-radius-md` (4px) for cards on extension pages, `sar-radius-pill` only for the switch track.
- No shadows on store pages except `sar-compact-shadow`, which keeps a compact badge's edge over mid-tone art, and the hover card's, which copies the store's own popup shadow. The banner is flat, like the store's notice bubbles. Cards on extension pages are flat.
- `sar-z-badge` (4) sits over capsule art, the store's corner flags and its hover microtrailer video (z-index 3), under its hover popups.
- `sar-z-hovercard` (2100) sits over the store's game hover popup (1526) and its React capsule hover layer (2000), for our hover card (section 15).

## Motion and focus

- Animate only color and opacity, with `sar-duration-fast` (120ms) or `sar-duration-base` (180ms) and `sar-ease`, and never on many elements at once. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` sets both durations to 0ms.
- The banner's collapse does not animate: a jump is calmer than a fake slide.
- Focus is a solid `sar-focus-width` (2px) ring in `sar-focus-color` at `sar-focus-offset` (2px), on `:focus-visible` only. It holds 3.69:1 or more on every ground, the banner wash included. Badges are not focusable; they ride inside the store's own link, whose focus opens the hover card (section 15).
- Forced colors: badges switch to `Canvas` and `CanvasText` with a visible border; the banner, whose wash disappears, gets a `CanvasText` edge.

## Longer translations and direction

- Badges stay on one line and truncate past their cap; the full label stays in the DOM text screen readers read, and the hover card shows it in full.
- Everything else wraps. Nothing that holds copy gets a fixed height; controls with copy have a minimum block size.
- Use logical properties (`padding-inline`, `inset-inline-end`, `border-inline-start`, `text-align: start`). The one physical value is the switch thumb's `translateX`. Right-to-left is out of scope for v1, but nothing here assumes left or right.
- No text in icons or images.

## Iconography

- **The mark** is a Steam badge, a ring around the Steam glyph, with a tag icon attached at its lower right: "Steam store pages, tagged". One ink per file, `sar-accent-400` in the extension icon; the tag leaves a transparent cut where it crosses the ring; no gradient, no letters. See section 14 for files, variants and sizes.
- **No icons in components.** Badges, the tag section and the banner carry no icon, like the store's own tags, corner flags and notice bubbles. The violet hue and the wording mark them as ours. The mark appears as the extension icon and wherever that icon is shown, never inside a component. Icons on chips for the other tag kinds are a later design decision (decision 39).
- No other icon set. Platform icons and flags around our badges are the store's.
- The extension icon holds an outlined Steam glyph (decision 40). It shows in the README logo, the store listing images, the options and popup headers and the design previews. No chip, badge, banner or hover card shows a Valve mark, and no Valve wordmark, image or font file ships anywhere.

## What renders for each state

The state is per kind: a game can be disclosed for one kind and not disclosed or unknown for another.

| Verdict of a kind | Chip | Tag section | Banner | List filter | Package summary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Disclosed | yes | a chip | an entry | dim or hide | counted, and in "of N" |
| Disclosed, ignored for the kind or for all tags | nothing | nothing | one-line note | nothing | in "of N" only |
| Not disclosed | nothing | nothing | nothing | nothing | in "of N" only |
| Unknown | nothing | nothing | nothing | nothing | left out of "of N" |

An unknown that holds for every kind with the reason `error` or `rate-limited` (a lookup that failed or was held back while the breaker was open) is asked again once the breaker has closed, at most twice. The chips, the tag section, the package summary and the banner then draw from the new answer, with no reload (decision 55). Until then nothing renders, as for any unknown.

A demo shows its full game's disclosed kinds beside its own, and its own disclosure of a kind wins (decision 59). A lent kind renders like the "Disclosed" row above on every surface; the hover card and the banner add `kind_full_game` under its heading. A kind whose full game is unknown is unknown for the demo too, unless the demo discloses it itself. A DLC lends nothing and borrows nothing.

A tag with values (Steam Deck, Steam Machine and Family Sharing, decisions 61 and 64) is tagged for a game only when its verdict is disclosed with a value the person ticked in `kinds.<id>.facets`. A disclosed value that is not ticked renders exactly like the "Not disclosed" row, on every surface, the list filter included, so Show, Dim and Hide act on the ticked values only. Valve's Unknown (0) is not disclosed, and a request that left out the `platforms` section leaves the kind unknown (`unrequested`), never Unsupported (decision 62). Family Sharing not Available is derived for a paid game only, and a free game, a DLC, a demo or an item without a feature list never shows it (decision 64). A Valve value lent to a demo reads `kind_full_game_valve` instead of `kind_full_game`.

A DLC follows its base game's ignore list. A kind disclosed on a DLC whose base game is ignored for that kind, or for all tags, renders as the "ignored" row above, except in the banner of the DLC's own page, which shows neither an entry nor a note for it (see "Ignored through a base game" below). Everything else about the state is per kind, as above.

Each kind's settings (`kinds.<id>`: `enabled`, `badge`, `banner`, `filter` and, for a tag with values, `facets`) gate its columns. `enabled` off removes everything for the kind, `badge` off removes its chips, in lists and in the tag section, and its line in the package summary, `banner` off removes its entry and its note, `filter` set to `off` leaves lists alone, and `facets` picks the values that count as tagged for all of them. All are on by default, except that PvP, In-App Purchases, Steam Deck and Steam Machine start with `enabled` off, Steam Deck and Steam Machine start with `banner` off and only Unsupported ticked, and the filter is `off`. The ignore list wins over every setting.

## Shared rules

**DOM ownership and state**

- Every injected element carries a class starting with `sar-`; every attribute we set on store nodes starts with `data-sar-`. The scanner skips both.
- Hosts get `data-sar-appid`, `data-sar-kinds` and, where the placement says hiding would break the list, `data-sar-hideable="false"`. `data-sar-kinds` holds the ids of the kinds that are disclosed and not ignored for the game, in registry order and separated by spaces, and is absent when there are none. Rules match one id with `~=`. The renderers write them when the scanner marks the host and again when a verdict, the ignore list or a setting changes, and only when the value changed.
- List filters live as attributes on `document.documentElement`, one per kind: `data-sar-filter-<kind>` (`off`, `dim`, `hide`). While the hidden results notice shows what Hide removed, the root also carries `data-sar-reveal` (section 16); nothing stores it. A kind that is switched off reads `off`. Chips carry `data-sar-kind` with the id of their kind. A kind's badge switch is not an attribute: the chips are drawn again from the settings, which also keeps the "+N" count right. The list filter is pure CSS on top of the attributes (section 5).
- Never move, wrap or restyle store nodes. The store-node changes besides the attributes above are `data-sar-anchor` on an image container that computes to `position: static`, which a CSS rule turns into `position: relative` (section 2), and a transient `aria-describedby` on a focused store link while the hover card is open (section 15).
- DOM reads (image sizes, computed position) are batched before writes in one `requestAnimationFrame` per scan.
- Listeners are delegated: one per root, never one per node. The tag section adds none: its chips are served by the hover card's listeners on the document (section 15).

**A tag's own color**

- The color is stored as `kinds.<id>.color`, a validated `#rrggbb`, and written on the root element as the custom property `--sar-kind-color-<id>`, one write per kind and none when it is unset (the property is removed). Chips, hover card entries and the banner carry `data-sar-kind`; one rule per kind reads that kind's property into `--sar-kind-color`, and a single rule derives the shades (`--sar-tag-edge`, `-tint`, `-text`, `-light`, `-solid`, `-wash-start`, `-wash-end`) from it. Each surface reads its shade with the violet token as the fallback, so a kind with no color renders exactly as before. Custom properties inherit into the banner's and the dialog's shadow roots, so they follow a change without a redraw.
- Only a validated `HexColor` is ever written; it never reaches CSS as typed text. Nothing is written on a chip, and a store node is never touched.

**Style isolation**

- Light-DOM components (both badges, the tag section and the hover card) reset with `all: unset` on themselves and their descendants, then set every property they need. Store CSS styles bare `span`, `a`, `h2` and `button` in places, and `all` leaves custom properties alone, so tokens still reach them.
- The banner renders into a shadow root on a host `div.sar-banner-host`. Page CSS cannot reach it, and its CSS cannot leak. Tokens inherit into it from `:root`, and the page's "Motiva Sans" works inside it because `@font-face` is document-wide. Attach the stylesheet with `adoptedStyleSheets`; if the store's CSP or a script manager blocks that, fall back to light DOM plus the badge reset.
- The userscript settings dialog uses the same shadow-root approach.

**i18n**

- Every visible string, `title`, `aria-label` and live-region message comes from `t(key, params)`. CSS carries no copy (no `content:` strings), and `options.html` carries `data-i18n` keys only.
- Data is never translated and never goes through the catalog: the developer's text, the AI service they named, the names Steam lists, game names and app IDs.
- Dates use `Intl.DateTimeFormat(locale, { dateStyle: "medium" })`. Plurals use `_one` and `_other` keys chosen with `Intl.PluralRules`. Language names in the language picker use `Intl.DisplayNames`, each in its own language, not catalog entries.
- Uppercase is CSS (`text-transform`), never catalog text, so it follows the page's `lang`.
- Sizing for longer translations is checked in the previews with Spanish copy and with labels up to three times the English length.

## 1. Full badge

The text chips that label a game's kinds on rows with room for words: search results, wishlist rows and DLC rows.

Preview: `previews/Badge.html`.

### When to use

- One chip for each kind that is disclosed, enabled, has its badge switch on and is not ignored for the game. Not disclosed, unknown and ignored kinds get nothing: no neutral chip, no check mark.
- The chips of a host sit in one group, in registry order. A placement shows at most its `maxChips` (3 on rows with room, 2 on capsule overlays and search suggestions), and one "+N" chip stands for the rest.
- A host with full chips gets no compact badge.

### What the consumer provides

```html
<span class="sar-chips" data-sar-chips="full">
  <span class="sar-badge" data-sar-kind="drm">
    <span class="sar-badge-label" aria-hidden="true">t('kind_drm_chip')</span>
    <span class="sar-vh">t('kind_chip_name', { chip, heading })</span>
  </span>
</span>
```

- The short text from `kind_<id>_chip` as real text, hidden from screen readers.
- A visually hidden name: the chip text, then Steam's line for the kind (`kind_chip_name`), such as "DRM: Incorporates 3rd-party DRM".
- The "+N" chip (`kind_chip_more`) carries no kind. Its name reads "+2: more tags" (`kind_chip_more_name`).
- Each chip registers with the hover card (section 15), which lists every kind of the host on hover or focus. No `title`.

### Look

Built like the store's own tag chips, in our hue.

- `sar-badge-height` (19px) tall, `sar-badge-pad-inline` (7px), `sar-radius-sm`, no border.
- Label in `sar-text-badge` (11/19 regular, the page font rather than the chips' Arial, because the badge sits in Motiva rows), `sar-badge-fg` on `sar-badge-bg`.
- No icon. The violet hue and the wording set it apart from the store's blue tags, and it never sits in the store's tag row.
- Capped at `sar-badge-max-inline-size` (180px). A longer translation ends in an ellipsis and keeps its full name in the DOM.
- The group sets its chips `sar-space-0` (2px) apart, like the store's own tag chips.

### Placement

- Search rows: directly after the title, with `margin-inline-start: sar-space-2`, keeping their full size. The title line clips with `overflow: hidden`, so on a row with chips it becomes a flex row: the title shrinks and ends in an ellipsis, and the chips stay visible. The platform icons below keep their place.
- Wishlist rows: directly after the title element, `margin-inline-start: sar-space-2`. The exact anchor is unverified until the surface spike.
- DLC rows on an app page: the last child of the name (`.game_area_dlc_name`), so the chips follow the name text. The row is a flex row that spreads name and price apart, so chips between them would float mid-row at a different spot on each row. On a row with chips the name becomes a flex row: its trailing space drops, and `margin-inline-start: sar-space-2` is the whole gap, as on search rows. A long name wraps beside the badge.
- A row placement picks the slot: `after` makes the group the anchor's next sibling, `end` its last child.
- Any other row: after the title, never before it, so titles stay aligned.

### States

- Default only; its look does not change on hover. The row's own hover shows through the tint and the label still clears 5.21:1. Resting on it opens the hover card.
- A kind whose badge switch is off draws no chip; the chips are drawn again when the setting changes.
- Inside a dimmed host they dim with the host.

### Accessibility

- The accessible name is the chip text followed by Steam's line for the kind, read as part of the row link. The short text itself is `aria-hidden`.
- No `role`, no `tabindex`: the chips ride inside the store's own link.

### Don't

- Don't say a game "uses AI", is "AI-free" or has "no AI", in any locale.
- Don't color it red, amber or green, and don't add a warning icon.

## 2. Compact badge

The overlay badge for capsule images, from 180px-wide capsules up to large header capsules, on an opaque surface so it reads over any art.

Preview: `previews/CompactBadge.html`.

### When to use

- A host with a kind to show whose capsule image is at least 180px wide and 32px tall. Smaller images get nothing on the image; a row-shaped host still gets full chips.
- One chip per kind in one group, at most two, then a "+N" chip. Never alongside full chips on the same host.

### What the consumer provides

```html
<span class="sar-chips" data-sar-chips="compact">
  <span class="sar-compact" data-sar-kind="drm" data-sar-size="text" data-sar-in-link="true">
    <span class="sar-compact-label" aria-hidden="true">t('kind_drm_chip')</span>
    <span class="sar-vh">t('kind_chip_name', { chip, heading })</span>
  </span>
</span>
```

- `data-sar-size` on each chip, set at placement from the measured image box (batched read, `getBoundingClientRect`). No container queries: they would change how store containers size themselves.
- `data-sar-in-link="true"` only when the group's `closest("a")` is the capsule link. The chips register with the hover card either way; outside a link no pointer reaches them.
- `data-sar-anchor` on the image container if it computes to `position: static`; the stylesheet turns it into `position: relative`. Never an inline style.

### Size tiers

| Tier | Image box | Renders | Height | Type | Inset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| none | under 180px wide or 32px tall | nothing | | | |
| `text` | 180 to 399px wide | label | `sar-compact-height` 19px | `sar-text-compact` 11/17 medium | `sar-compact-inset-block` 5px, `sar-compact-inset-inline` 5px |
| `large` | 400px wide and 150px tall | label | `sar-compact-height-lg` 22px | `sar-text-compact-lg` 12/20 medium | same |

Tiers depend on image size only. A longer translation truncates inside its tier and never moves the badge to another.

### Look and placement

Built as a mirror of the store's own corner flags (in library, on wishlist): solid, square, 19px tall, and 5px from the top and 5px from the end edge, so the two margins match.

- `sar-compact-fg` on `sar-compact-bg` (12.65:1 whatever the art), square corners, a 1px `sar-compact-border` and `sar-compact-shadow`.
- Unlike the store's mid-tone flags, the surface is dark (accent 900) and keeps an edge. Together they give every kind of art a boundary at 3:1 or more: the edge against black (5.91:1), the surface against white (16.63:1), mid grey (4.21:1) and violet (3.34:1).
- Block-start, inline-end corner (top right in English), mirroring the store's flags, which sit top-left. The store also owns the bottom edge (prices).
- `z-index: sar-z-badge`: over the art, the corner flags and the microtrailer video that home capsules play on hover, under the store's hover popups.
- Maximum inline size is the image width less the inline inset (the group is anchored at the end edge only: the inset is subtracted once, and the start edge may be reached); past it the chips shrink and their text ends in an ellipsis. Two chips can take more room than the one label did before, and the badges share the store flags' row, so a longer translation on a small capsule can reach a store flag; see the open questions.
- On home capsules, the store's options arrow (`.ds_options`, z-index 15) appears in the same corner on hover and covers about 39px of the group's inline end before the inset change, probably about 34px after (an estimate, not measured on a live page). Curator pages (`store.steampowered.com/curator/<id>/`) carry the same kind of three-dots control in that corner, beside or over the chips. The store control stays on top on purpose and there is no workaround for now (known issue, decision 33); at rest on home the chips are clear.

### Pointer and accessibility

- Inside the capsule link: `pointer-events: auto`, so hovering or clicking the badge is hovering or clicking the link, and the hover card opens. Focus on the link opens it too.
- Outside a link: `pointer-events: none`, so it never swallows a click on an overlaid link, and no hover card opens. The label stays in the DOM.

### Don't

- No translucent or blurred surface: contrast would depend on the art, and blur costs paint time on carousel pages.
- No rounded corners: the store's flags are square.
- Not bottom-left: cover art usually puts the game's logo there.

## 3. Tag section

A block on a game's own store page, directly below Steam's "Popular user-defined tags for this product:" block: a small muted heading, "Tag Kit tags for this product", and one violet chip for each kind the game discloses. It is laid out like Steam's tag row, and the heading and the hue keep it apart from the players' tags.

Preview: `previews/TagSection.html`.

### When to use

- On the app page of a game with a disclosed kind that is enabled, not ignored for the game (for the kind or for all tags) and has its badge switch on. These are the filters of the chips on lists. Each kind counts on its own: switching off the badge of one kind, or ignoring the game for it, removes only that chip.
- All chips show, in registry order, and wrap to more rows. There is no "+N" chip. A game whose only declaration is kernel-level anti-cheat gets a section with one chip.
- With no chip to show, there is no section and no heading.
- Once per page. The banner, lower down by the purchase box, still carries the developer's words and the ignore buttons.

### What the consumer provides

```html
<div class="sar-tag-section" role="group" aria-labelledby="sar-tag-section-label">

  <span class="sar-chips" data-sar-chips="full">
    <span class="sar-badge" data-sar-kind="ai" tabindex="0">
      <span class="sar-badge-label" aria-hidden="true">t('kind_ai_chip')</span>
      <span class="sar-vh">t('kind_chip_name', { chip, heading })</span>
    </span>
    <!-- one chip for each further kind -->
  </span>
</div>
```

- Insert it right after the store's `.popular_tags_ctn` (the "Popular user-defined tags" block in the summary box). If the page has no such block, insert it after the last `.dev_row` (Developer or Publisher) of the summary box, and if it has neither, draw nothing. The first anchor that matches wins and the section moves there when it appears later. The class names come from the store's page as known from memory, not from a captured page (no fixture holds the summary box); the fallback keeps the section on screen if the tag block is renamed. It uses its own classes and never borrows or restyles a store node.
- The chips are the full badge (`createChip`) in a `sar-chips` group, the same markup lists use, so the accessible name, the reset and the wrapping rule are shared. Each chip registers with the hover card with its own verdict and `ownPage` set (section 15).

### Look

Steam's tag row is a wrapping row of 19px chips 2px apart in Arial 11, under a small muted label. This block matches it and swaps the blue for our hue.

- The heading: `sar-text-summary-tag` in `sar-color-text-muted`, with no colon and `sar-space-1` below it. The block sits `sar-space-2` below the store's tag block.
- The chips: the full badge (`sar-badge-fg` on `sar-badge-bg`, 19px, `sar-radius-sm`), set in `sar-text-summary-tag` like the store's tag chips, `sar-space-0` (2px) apart in both directions. ### Fit

The label's size, color and spacing are set from the tokens, not sampled from the live label. Measure on a page with and without user tags (open questions 14 to 16 in `decisions.mdx`).

### Accessibility

- The group is named by its visible heading. Each chip's name is the chip text and Steam's heading, "AI: AI Generated Content Disclosure" (PvP reads "PvP" once).
- Each chip is focusable and opens its card on focus, which describes the chip while it is open.

### Don't

- Don't add the chips to Steam's tag row: that row never wraps, Steam's own script hides tags that don't fit, and a chip there would read as a player-defined tag.
- Don't float it over the page.
- Don't add a "+N" chip or a warning icon; the chip is the only violet.

## 4. App page banner

A non-blocking notice on a game's own store page that lists every kind the developer declared, in Steam's words, and quotes the developer's text as written. It sits above the purchase box and is built like the store's own notice bubbles.

Preview: `previews/Banner.html`.

### When to use

- On the app page of a game with at least one disclosed kind that is enabled and has its banner switch on, once per page view. One banner holds every such kind, in registry order.
- A kind the game is ignored for is not listed. It becomes a one-line note with Undo. A game ignored for all tags gets one such note and nothing else.
- Below the entries sits a closed section, "Ignore a developer or publisher", with a row for each of the game's developers and publishers (see "Ignoring a developer or publisher" below). A tag a creator is ignored for becomes a note with Undo.
- On a demo's page, a kind the full game declared and the demo did not is listed like the others, with `kind_full_game` right under its heading and before the developer's text, which stays the full game's developer's (decision 59). A Valve value lent this way reads `kind_full_game_valve`.
- A tag with values is headed by `kind_facet_name`, Steam's heading and the value's word ("Steam Deck Compatibility: Unsupported"), or by Steam's word alone for a value with a chip of its own ("Family Sharing not Available"), and "Ignore for this tag" ignores the kind, every value.
- A kind the game's base game is ignored for (the game is a DLC) is not listed and gets no note, since its Undo would belong to the other game's page. A DLC page whose every kind is silenced this way has no banner. "Ignore this game" on a DLC page still ignores that DLC only.
- Never as a modal or overlay, never over content, never stealing focus.

### What the consumer provides

The banner is a summary, one line per kind: Steam's line and its ignore button. - Developer text is data: `textContent`, never translated, summarized or highlighted. The notes take `white-space: pre-line`.
- The banner draws no link, so a EULA appears by its heading only; the agreement names are in the hover card.

- A shadow root on `div.sar-banner-host`, styles attached with `adoptedStyleSheets`. Tokens inherit from `:root`, and the page font works inside because `@font-face` is document-wide.

### Placement

First child of the left description column, directly above the purchase box: where a buyer looks before buying. Full column width, about 616 to 643px on desktop and down to about 360px in the store's narrow layout. Re-inserted only if a store re-render removes it.

### Look

Modeled on the store's per-app notice bubbles (the DLC notice sits in the same slot): a hue-coded wash and nothing else. Every per-app notice on the store is a gradient in its own hue, so a colored bubble is the native idiom; our hue keeps it from passing as the store's own content.

- Background `sar-banner-bg`: a 135 degree wash from `sar-banner-wash-start` at 10% to `sar-banner-wash-end` at 100% (accent 800 to accent 600, both at 80%), the DLC bubble's recipe in our hue.
- No border, no radius, no shadow.
- Padding `sar-space-2` at block-start, `sar-space-3` at block-end, `sar-space-4` inline. `sar-space-4` below: the store's own 2px would glue the banner to the purchase box and read as part of the offer.
- All text in `sar-banner-fg`. Heading `sar-text-banner-title` (24/30 light) when the banner holds one entry. With two or more, the headings step down to 18/23 light (`sar-size-2xl`) and the entries sit `sar-space-3` apart. Detail, source and service lines `sar-text-banner-meta` (13/19 light). Notes `sar-text-banner-quote` (14/21, italic like the notes in the store's own disclosure section).
- "Ignore for this tag" matches the store's outlined buttons right below the banner ("Compare Editions", "Browse all"): `sar-text-banner-button` (11/14 regular, the store's size) in `sar-banner-button-fg`, `sar-banner-button-pad-block` (2px) and `sar-banner-button-pad-inline` (15px), a 1px `sar-banner-button-border` (white at 40%), `sar-radius-sm`, a `sar-banner-button-bg-hover` wash on hover. It stays at least `sar-control-height-xs` (24px) tall for the target size (WCAG 2.5.8), where the store's buttons are 22px. It sits at inline-end of its entry's heading.
- "Ignore this game", "Show full report" and "Undo" are the store's underlined in-bubble links, in `sar-banner-link-color`, as `button` elements with a 24px minimum target. "Ignore this game" stands alone at inline-end under the last entry. A EULA link looks the same, as an anchor.
- Errors in `sar-banner-error`, never `sar-color-error` (4.21:1 on the bright end).
- Never `sar-color-text-muted` or `sar-color-text-subtle` on the wash.

### States

- **Collapsed** (default when the AI notes are long): the quote clamps to `sar-banner-quote-lines` (3). Measure in the next frame; if it does not overflow, drop `data-sar-collapsed` and omit the link.
- **Expanded:** the link reads `banner_collapse` with `aria-expanded="true"`. No animation; not remembered across pages.
- **A kind ignored:** its entry gives way to one line in the banner's own type, `banner_ignored_kind` and an Undo link, after the entries that remain.
- **Only notes left** (`data-sar-variant="ignored"`): the store's DRM-notice geometry in neutral, no violet. The note lines (`banner_ignored` for all tags, one `banner_ignored_kind` for each kind) in `sar-text-banner-note` and `sar-color-text-muted`, each with an Undo link, on `sar-banner-note-bg` with `sar-radius-xs`, `sar-space-1` by `sar-space-2` padding, labelled by `banner_region_label`, or by `badge_tags_title` when an entry comes from Valve.
- **Storage error:** the banner stays as it was and shows `error_allow_list_save` until the next successful action.
- **Setting turned off:** the kind's entry and note leave, and the host leaves when nothing is left. Turned on: re-render from the known verdicts, no new request.

### Behavior

- "Ignore for this tag" adds the app ID (with the name and today's date) to that kind's list and swaps its entry for the note; focus moves to the note's Undo. "Ignore this game" adds the app ID to the list for all tags, and every entry gives way to one note. Neither calls the store's own ignore feature.
- "Undo" removes the app ID from that list again and restores the entries. Focus returns to the ignore button of the same scope, or to "Ignore this game" after an undo for all tags.
- Chips on the game's capsules follow the same lists, so they leave and return with the entries.

#### Ignoring a developer or publisher

A native `details` under the entries, closed on every load (decision 13) and open across a redraw, with `banner_creators_toggle` as its `summary`. It lists one row per name and role from `basic_info`: the name, as text, then the role in muted text (`banner_role_developer`, `banner_role_publisher`), then a link-style button for each tag the banner lists that the creator is not yet ignored for (`banner_ignore_creator_kind`, with the tag's short label) and `banner_ignore_creator_all`. A company that develops and publishes has a row for each role, and each row ignores that role only. A button is named by its text and the row's name and role. A creator without a clan ID is keyed by its name (`n:<name>`), one with an ID by `c:<id>`.

A tag silenced through a creator is a note with Undo (`banner_ignored_creator_kind`), and a creator ignored for all tags is one note (`banner_ignored_creator`), after the game's own notes. A creator note shows only while the banner would list a tag it covers. After an ignore focus lands on the note's Undo, after an Undo on the button it brought back (the summary when none is left). The polite live region announces `notice_creator_stopped`, `notice_creator_stopped_kind`, `notice_creator_restored` or `notice_creator_restored_kind`. A failed save shows `error_creator_list_save` in the error line.

The two lists are shown in the options page, the popup and the userscript dialog as "Developers you ignored" and "Publishers you ignored", after "Games you ignored", with the same groups and "Stop ignoring" button. The popup leaves out an empty card, and an empty card on the page reads `options_allow_empty` with the section's label as the action.

#### Ignored through a base game

A DLC is ignored for a kind when its own app ID or its base game's app ID is on that kind's list or on the list for all tags. The base game is `related_items.parent_appid` of the DLC's GetItems item, kept with the cached verdict as `parent`; no list of DLC is stored, so a DLC released tomorrow follows at once. `isIgnored` (`src/core/ignored.ts`) is the only place that decides; the chips, the `data-sar-kinds` mark (so the dim and hide filters), the hover card and the app page's banner and tag section all ask it. The "Games you ignored" list is keyed by the games the person ignored, so a DLC never appears in it, and "Stop ignoring" on the base game brings the DLC back. A parent that is not known (not fetched yet, no parent, a source that does not carry it) leaves the game on its own entries. The relation is one level. A demo follows its full game the same way, for its own kinds and for the kinds its full game lends it (decision 59).
- The polite live region announces `notice_marking_stopped` or `notice_marking_restored`.

### Longer translations

The head wraps: the heading takes `flex: 1 1 12em` and wraps first (at 24px a longer translation, such as Spanish, already wraps at 616px), then the button moves to its own line at inline-end. The buttons and links wrap their own text. In a note the text and the link wrap inside their own boxes.

### Accessibility

An `aside` landmark. With one entry and no note it is labelled by that entry's `h2`; otherwise by `banner_region_label`, or by `badge_tags_title` ("Tag Kit tags for this product") when any entry comes from Valve, since "Declared by the developer" would be false for it (decision 63). Every entry has its own `h2`. Each "Ignore for this tag" button takes its name from its text and the entry's heading, and each Undo from its text and its note, so no two buttons share a name. The notes are a `blockquote`; the source line names the speaker. No `lang` on the quote, since the developer's language is unknown. Every control is a real `button` with visible text. In forced colors the wash disappears, so the banner gets a `CanvasText` edge.

### Check

A game with DLC can also carry a report, so this banner may stack with the store's own DLC bubble. Compare the two side by side on such a page.

## 5. Dim and hide (list filter)

The opt-in dim or hide mode for lists, one per kind, written as pure CSS on attributes so a setting change is one write per kind, however many games a page holds.

Preview: `previews/ListFilter.html`.

### What the consumer provides

- On `document.documentElement`, for each kind: `data-sar-filter-<kind>="off" | "dim" | "hide"` (default `off`).
- On each host, written by the renderer: `data-sar-kinds` (the ids of the kinds that are disclosed and not ignored for the game, separated by spaces) and, where hiding would break a virtualized or carousel list, `data-sar-hideable="false"`.

No classes, no inline styles, no per-node writes when the mode changes.

### Behavior

- `off`: lists unchanged; chips only.
- `dim`: hosts that carry the kind get `filter: opacity(sar-dim-opacity)` (0.4). `:hover` and `:focus-within` restore full opacity on that one host with a `sar-duration-fast` fade in and a snap back.
- `hide`: hosts that carry the kind get `display: none`. Hosts marked `data-sar-hideable="false"` dim instead, as does every host while the root carries `data-sar-reveal`, which the hidden results notice sets for one page (section 16).
- Several kinds on one host: the strongest mode wins (hide over dim over off). No rule is needed for it: a hidden host ignores a dim, and a host that cannot hide dims for either mode.
- A kind that is not disclosed, unknown or ignored for the game is absent from `data-sar-kinds`, so it never matches. A kind that is switched off writes `off` for its filter.

```css
/* One pair of rules for each kind; drm shown */
[data-sar-filter-drm="dim"] [data-sar-kinds~="drm"],
[data-sar-filter-drm="hide"]
  [data-sar-kinds~="drm"]:is([data-sar-hideable="false"], [data-sar-reveal] *) {
  filter: opacity(var(--sar-dim-opacity)) !important;
}
[data-sar-filter-drm="hide"]:not([data-sar-reveal])
  [data-sar-kinds~="drm"]:not([data-sar-hideable="false"]) {
  display: none !important;
}
```

### Rules

- A filter opacity only. It multiplies with the store's own `opacity`, while the `opacity` property would replace it: the home page's main carousel hides its other slides with `opacity: 0`, and an `opacity` dim made them show through. It costs one effect per dimmed host, as `opacity` does. Other filters such as grayscale cost more; a sale page holds hundreds of capsules, so profile before adding one.
- No transition on a mode change, so every host flips in one style pass.
- `!important` is required: store rows set `display` inline or with higher specificity.
- The rules list each kind by hand, because no selector joins an attribute on the root element with one on a host. `ui/styles.test.ts` fails for a kind they miss.
- The store's own ignored state is 0.3. Ours is 0.4, and the chips on the host name the reason.
- Dimmed content is inactive UI: exempt from contrast minimums, never the only route to information.
- Known gap: the filter can apply after first paint, because the content script runs at `document_idle`.
- On an app page, dim and hide reach card-sized hosts only. The page's header capsule, purchase area and description name the page's app, which keeps a host for another game (the base game a DLC page links to) from taking them in, and links in the DLC bubble and the breadcrumbs make no host. The page's own app is never a host on its page, and its header card does not dim by its own tags (decision 56).

## 6. Options page and action popup

The settings form, rendered as the full options page (`data-sar-context="page"`) and as the toolbar popup (`data-sar-context="popup"`), dark only. The stored settings are `locale`, `debug` and, for each kind, `kinds.<id>.enabled`, `kinds.<id>.badge`, `kinds.<id>.banner`, `kinds.<id>.filter` and, when the person set one, `kinds.<id>.color`.

Preview: `previews/OptionsPage.html`.

### What the consumer provides

- `options.html` with structure and `data-i18n` keys only; every string from the catalog.
- The context attribute on `.sar-options`. The userscript mounts the same form in its settings dialog.
- Whether to show the color fields (`colors`). It is a capability apart from the context, so the dialog keeps the popup layout and still gets them: the full page and the dialog pass it, the toolbar popup does not.
- The version from `package.json` for `options_version`.

### Layout

- Page: centered, `sar-options-max-width` (640px), `sar-space-4` padding, `sar-color-bg-panel`, `color-scheme: dark`.
- Popup: `sar-popup-width` (360px), `sar-space-3` padding, short labels where a key exists. With a section for each of ten kinds the popup is tall and scrolls. Sections are flat, not cards: no fill, radius or inline padding, `sar-space-2` above and below, and a 1px `sar-color-divider` line between them. Section titles become captions in `sar-text-popup-caption` (11/16 medium, uppercase) and `sar-color-text-subtle`, like the store's own section labels. Rows take `sar-space-1` padding, the segmented control takes `sar-control-height-xs` (24px, the store's smallest control), and the allow-list scrolls inside 240px. An empty allow-list is left out of the popup; the full page keeps its empty state.
- Font: the `ext` family (a system stack). The store font is not loaded on extension pages and none is bundled.
- Type on the full page: field labels, help and error lines in `sar-text-body` (13/18). The popup keeps help and error lines in `sar-text-help` (12/16).
- On the full page, sections are `.sar-card`: `sar-color-bg-raised`, `sar-radius-md`, `sar-space-3` padding, `sar-space-2` apart, titled in `sar-text-card-title` with `sar-space-2` below the title.
- Each tag's section closes. It is a native `details` with its `summary` first: the section title (still the `h2`, still focusable with `tabindex="-1"` so focus can land on it) and a cue, `options_state_on` or `options_state_off`, from the master switch, in `sar-text-help` (`sar-color-text` when on, `sar-color-text-subtle` when off). The cue is words, so it never relies on color. After the cue comes an arrow drawn in CSS, which points down when closed and up when open. The summary is at least `sar-control-height-sm` tall (`sar-control-height-xs` in the popup) and takes the same focus ring as the other controls. There is no animation. The other cards (language, ignored games, backup, privacy) do not close.
- Header: the 32px icon (48px source), `ext_name` in `sar-text-options-title`, the version in `sar-color-text-subtle`.

### Sections, in order

1. Header.
2. One section for each tag kind, in registry order (PvP, In-App Purchases, Steam Deck, Steam Machine and Family Sharing start switched off, the other kinds on), titled with Steam's line for the kind (`kind_<id>_heading`). It holds the master switch `options_kind_enabled` (`kinds.<id>.enabled`), then, for a tag with values, a group of native checkboxes, one per value in Steam's words (Verified, Playable, Unsupported, or Family Sharing and Family Sharing not Available; `facets`; label `options_facets_label`, help `options_facets_help`), on the full page, in the popup and in the userscript dialog alike, since the choice decides what the tag means, then the switches `options_badges` (popup `options_badges_short`; `badge`) and `options_banner` (`banner`), then, on the full page and in the userscript dialog only, the color field (below), then a stacked SegmentedControl with Show, Dim and Hide (`filter`; values `off`, `dim`, `hide`; label `options_filter_label`; the help follows the selection). Each switch has help. While the master switch is off, the other three are hidden and their stored values stay. The summary shows the title and the master switch's state; the master switch and the rest sit in the body, one open away.
3. Language (page only): a stacked Select, `Automatic` first, then each shipped locale named in its own language through `Intl.DisplayNames`.
4. Games you ignored: the AllowList, in groups. All tags comes first, then each kind in registry order, each group titled with its scope's name and listing its games newest first. Left out of the popup while it is empty.
5. Backup (full page and userscript dialog, not the toolbar popup): the Backup card, to download a file and restore from one.
6. Privacy (page only): four bullets.
7. Footer: `options_unaffiliated` in `sar-text-help`, subtle. The popup uses `options_unaffiliated_short` and adds an `options_all_settings` link.

### Behavior

- Every change applies at once through the settings port; there is no save button. It is a patch of the fields the control names, applied to the settings stored at that moment, so a page that holds older settings never overwrites another kind's values. A polite live region announces `notice_settings_saved`.
- A failed write reverts the control and shows `error_settings_save` under it in `.sar-field-error` (`role="alert"`).
- A failed read shows the defaults with `error_settings_load` above the first section, outside every `details`, so a closed section cannot hide it.
- A section starts open. Closing or opening one is remembered for that section alone, in the `v2:ui` record (`{ "open": { "<id>": false } }`, only sections the person has toggled), so the popup, the full page and the userscript dialog agree. It is interface state, not a setting, and it is not in the backup. A record that cannot be read or has bad entries leaves the section open; a write that fails loses only the memory of the choice, with no error shown. One listener on the form's root, in the capture phase, hears every section's `toggle` event, because `toggle` does not bubble.

### The color field (experimental)

One stacked field in each tag's options, after the banner switch and before the list filter, where the form has color fields. It sets `kinds.<id>.color`.

## 12. Backup

Saves what the person set to a file they hold, and restores it after a reinstall, in another browser, or from the extension to the userscript and back: there are no accounts and no server copy, so the file is the backup. Built as decision 54 describes; `ui/backup-card.ts` draws it, `backup/` holds the format and the restore.

Preview: `previews/Backup.html`.

### What a backup holds

- Settings: each tag's switches, list filter and color, the language and the debug flag.
- Games, developers and publishers you ignored, by scope, with their names and dates.

Not the lookup history, the open sections (`v2:ui`) or anything else derived. One JSON file named `ai-reporter-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.json` (local date), with `"format": "sar-backup"` and a `version`. The `ai-reporter-` prefix is a deliberate technical identifier that stays when the product name changes, like the `sar-` prefix. The file reuses the stored shapes of `v2:settings` and `v2:allow` under names of its own, so the extension and the userscript read the same file. Nothing else goes in: no cookies, no account data, nothing from the store.

### Where it appears

- The full options page: its own card, after "Games you ignored" and before "Privacy".
- The userscript settings dialog: the same card, with its file input inside the dialog.
- Not the toolbar popup: opening a file picker can close an extension popup before the file arrives. The popup's "All settings" link leads to the full page.

### What the consumer provides

```html
<section class="sar-card" aria-labelledby="backup-title">
  <h2 class="sar-card-title" id="backup-title">t('options_section_backup')</h2>
  <p class="sar-backup-help">t('options_backup_help')</p>
  <div class="sar-backup-actions">
    <button class="sar-button" type="button">t('options_backup_download')</button>
    <button class="sar-button" type="button">t('options_backup_restore')</button>
  </div>

  <!-- Only after a valid file is chosen -->
  <div class="sar-backup-summary" role="group" aria-labelledby="backup-summary-title">
    <p class="sar-backup-summary-title" id="backup-summary-title" tabindex="-1">
      t('options_backup_summary_title', { date })
    </p>
    <ul class="sar-backup-contents">
      <li>t('options_backup_summary_list', { list: t('options_section_allow'), number })</li>
      <li>t('options_backup_summary_list', { list: t('options_section_allow_developers'), number })</li>
      <li>t('options_backup_summary_list', { list: t('options_section_allow_publishers'), number })</li>
      <!-- Only when entries cannot be read -->
      <li>plural('options_backup_summary_skipped', count)</li>
    </ul>
    <p class="sar-backup-note">t('options_backup_restore_note')</p>
    <div class="sar-backup-actions" data-sar-align="end">
      <button class="sar-button" data-sar-variant="primary" type="button">t('options_backup_confirm')</button>
      <button class="sar-button" type="button">t('options_backup_cancel')</button>
    </div>
  </div>

  <p class="sar-backup-status" hidden>plural('notice_backup_restored', added)</p>
  <p class="sar-field-error" role="alert" hidden>t('error_backup_invalid', { name })</p>
</section>
```

- "Restore from a backup" is a real `button` that asks the `FileTransfer` port to open the chooser, inside the click. The port keeps one hidden `input type=file` (`accept=".json,application/json"`, named `options_backup_restore` by `aria-label`) in the page body, or in the dialog for the userscript. A styled `label` cannot take keyboard focus.
- Counts and the date come from the file after it is validated; the date is formatted with `Intl.DateTimeFormat(locale, { dateStyle: "medium" })`. The skipped count uses plural keys (`_one`, `_other`).
- Results go to the status line and the form's polite live region; errors to the card's `.sar-field-error`.

### Flow

**Download**

1. "Download backup" builds the file from storage and starts the download (an `a[download]` on a Blob URL, revoked 30 seconds later). The browser's own download UI confirms the file; the status line shows `notice_backup_downloaded` and the live region announces it.
2. If the file cannot be built or the browser refuses it: `error_backup_create`. Nothing else changes.

**Restore**

1. "Restore from a backup" opens the file picker.
2. The file is untrusted data: cap its size at 1 MiB, parse it as JSON to `unknown`, check the format and version, read the settings and lists with the stored schemas, and never render any of its text as HTML. Names go in with `textContent`.
3. Not a backup, or over the cap: `error_backup_invalid`. From a newer format: `error_backup_newer`. Unreadable: `error_backup_read`. Each names the file. Nothing changes in any of these.
4. Valid: the summary opens in place, inside the card. No modal. Focus moves to its title, so a screen reader hears the date and contents.
5. "Restore" merges the ignore lists (a union; the earlier date wins where both list an entry) and then replaces the settings in one patch. Open store tabs update their settings through the settings subscription, as for any settings change, and their lists on the next load.
6. Afterwards the summary closes, the status line reads `notice_backup_restored` with the number of new entries, then `notice_backup_skipped` when entries were left out, the live region announces it, and focus returns to "Restore from a backup".
7. If a write fails: `error_backup_restore`, and the summary stays open. Only additions can have landed; a retry is safe.
8. "Cancel" closes the summary and returns focus to "Restore from a backup".

### Look

- Help text in `sar-text-help` and `sar-color-text-subtle`.
- Actions: a wrapping row of buttons, `sar-space-2` apart. Long labels wrap inside their buttons, and buttons drop to the next line when the row is too narrow.
- The check's Restore and Cancel row sits on the right, Restore first, `sar-space-3` apart, like the store's own confirm dialogs (`.newmodal_buttons`: right-aligned, the confirm button first, 12px apart).
- Summary: `sar-color-bg-sunken`, 1px `sar-color-border`, `sar-radius-md`, `sar-space-3` padding. Title in `sar-color-text-strong`, medium. Contents in `sar-color-text`. The note in `sar-text-help` and `sar-color-text-subtle`.
- "Restore" is the one primary button in the product (`data-sar-variant="primary"`): it confirms the only action that overwrites settings.
- The status line uses `sar-text-help` in `sar-color-text`. Errors use `.sar-field-error`.
- No green for success and no red fill for failure: words carry the state.

### Don't

- Don't restore the moment a file is picked. Always show the summary first.
- Don't send the file anywhere or read it on a server.
- Don't put a backup control on store pages or in the popup.

## 14. Extension icon

Built like the store's game hover popup, sampled on the home page on 2026-10-21: a `#344352` panel, no radius, a 20px black shadow at 50%, 16px of padding, 306px wide, set in the page font.

- Each entry's title is Steam's line for the kind, in `sar-hovercard-title-font` (15/17, like the store's game name there), `sar-badge-fg`. The violet title is what marks the card as ours. Entries sit `sar-space-2` apart.
- Detail lines (names, Steam's notes, category names) in `sar-hovercard-meta-font` (11/16) and `sar-color-text`.
- The full game line, `kind_full_game`, in the meta font and `sar-color-text-muted`, like the source line.
- The source lines, `badge_card_declared` and `badge_card_valve`, end the card in `sar-hovercard-meta-font` (11/16) and `sar-color-text-muted`, like the store's release line.
- Notes in `sar-hovercard-notes-font` (12/18 italic, like the store's own disclosure section), `sar-color-text`, `white-space: pre-line`, clamped to `sar-hovercard-notes-lines` (6) lines.
- Service line in the meta font: label `sar-color-text-muted`, name `sar-color-text`.
- The "more" line, `badge_card_more` in the meta font and `sar-color-text-muted`, shows only when the clamp hides text and the trigger is not on the game's own page. The app page carries Steam's own disclosure section and, when on, the banner; a tag-section chip's card never shows the line. The full notes stay in the DOM for screen readers, and the game's store page carries them in Steam's own disclosure section.
- No arrow. The store's popup has a bitmap arrow toward the capsule; ours sits right under the chip, and the short gap ties it to its trigger.

### Placement

- Fixed to the viewport, `sar-hovercard-offset` (8px) below the trigger. Where it would cross the bottom edge and fits above, it flips above (`data-sar-side="above"`).
- Horizontally, a full chip and a tag-section chip align the card's inline-start edge with their own. A compact chip aligns the inline-end edges: the card hangs back over the capsule art, away from the side where the store's popup opens. Either way the card stays one offset inside the viewport edges.
- `z-index: sar-z-hovercard` (2100), above the store's game hover popup (1526) and the full-viewport layer that React store pages (tag hubs, categories) mount for their capsule hover (2000). The card is also a manual popover, shown again on every open, so it enters the browser's top layer after any store popover that is open; the React hover layer is one, and no z-index reaches above it. The z-index covers engines without the Popover API.
- While the card is open, the root element carries `data-sar-hovercard="open"`, and one page rule sets the store's game hover popup (`#global_hover`) to `visibility: hidden`. On a search row the badge sits inside the row's link, so without it both would open for one pointer. Closing the card, `pagehide` and stopping remove the attribute, and the store's popup shows again while the pointer is still on the row.
- On React store pages (tag hubs, categories) the store's hover layer covers the capsule as soon as the pointer enters it and takes the pointer, so the pointer cannot rest on the compact badge there. Focus on the capsule link still opens the card. The layer has no stable selector (a hashed class and `popover="manual"`, which our card shares), so no rule hides it.
- The card covers store content only while it is open, and only in answer to a pointer or focus on our own element. It never opens by itself.

### Behavior

- Pointer: opens after 150ms at rest on a trigger. A pointer crossing a grid of capsules opens nothing. Moving to another trigger while it is open switches at once.
- The card is hoverable: leaving the trigger starts a 100ms grace, long enough for the pointer to cross onto the card, which then stays open. Leaving both, or the window, closes it. Both delays are timers in `hover-card.ts`, not tokens.
- Keyboard: opens at once when focus lands on a tag-section chip or on a store link that holds a chip (a search row or a capsule link). Moving focus elsewhere closes it.
- `Escape` closes it, and it stays closed until the pointer leaves the trigger or focus moves.
- Any scroll outside the card closes a card the pointer opened; a fixed card would drift away from its trigger. A card focus opened follows its trigger instead, because focus scrolls a link into view: the one delegated scroll listener queues at most one move per frame, which reads only the trigger's box, and the card closes once the trigger leaves the viewport.

### Accessibility

- `role="tooltip"` on the card. While focus opened it, the focused element gets `aria-describedby="sar-hovercard"`, added to any ids already there and restored on close. On a store link this is the one store-node attribute outside `data-sar-*`, and it lasts only while the card is open.
- A tag-section chip is focusable (`tabindex="0"`), so keyboard users reach its card.
- Badges stay unfocusable; focus rides on the store's link around them.
- WCAG 2.2 content on hover or focus (1.4.13): dismissible with `Escape`, hoverable, and open until the pointer or focus leaves.

### Motion

- Fades in over `sar-duration-fast` with `sar-ease` through `@starting-style`; closing is instant. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` sets the duration to 0ms and the card appears without a fade.
- No movement: position and size never animate.

### Don't

- Don't add a link or button inside the card. Keyboard users never reach it, because focus stays on the trigger.
- Don't fetch anything to fill it.
- Don't add per-badge listeners or a card per badge.

## 16. Hidden results notice

A bar above a list, in the look of the store's own notice bar above search results, that says how many titles the list filter hid there, with a link-styled button that shows them on this page and hides them again. The setting stays as it is.

### When to use

- On a list surface named in `LIST_PLACEMENTS` (`scan/placements.ts`): search results first. A new surface is one entry: the selector of the element that holds its hosts and, when the store shows its own notice above that list, the selector of that notice in `after`.
- Only while at least one host in that list is hidden: a kind on `hide`, the host carries it and the host can hide. A host that dims instead does not count.

### What the consumer provides

- The marks of section 5: `data-sar-filter-<kind>` on the root element, `data-sar-kinds` and `data-sar-hideable` on the hosts. The notice writes no host and reads no setting.
- The count, from the plural keys `notice_list_hidden` and `notice_list_revealed`, and the button text from `notice_list_show` and `notice_list_hide`.

### Look

- The store's notice bar, sampled on the live search page on 2026-10-26 (decision 52): `sar-store-notice-bg` (black at 20%), `sar-store-notice-fg` (`#fafad2`), `sar-store-notice-pad` (10px on every side), `sar-store-notice-font` (12px Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif at a normal line height), no radius, no border, and `sar-store-notice-margin-block-end` (5px) below it. No violet: the bar reports what the extension did, not a tag.
- Full width of the list's column, like the store's bar. The store's gear cell at the end is left out: our bar has no menu and components carry no icons.
- The button is an underlined link inside the sentence, in `sar-store-notice-fg`, like the store's own "here" link in its bar, 4px after the text. On hover it takes `sar-store-notice-link-hover`, the store's usual link hover, which was not sampled on the bar. No minimum block size: an inline link in a sentence is exempt from the target size rule, and the bar keeps the store's height.
- It goes right after the store's own bar (`#search_results_filtered_warning_persistent`) when the page has one, else right before the list element, under the store's sort bar. It wraps before it truncates.
- One node of our own, never inside the store's bar and never with its classes or ids, which would pull Valve's CSS onto ours. Light DOM with the badge reset, like the other page components.

### Behavior

- A click sets `data-sar-reveal` on the root element: the hide rules skip it and those hosts dim (section 5). The bar then reads "4 hidden titles are showing." and the button "Hide them". The next click removes it.
- Nothing is stored. A reload, a new list element from the store, `pagehide`, stopping, or the count reaching zero ends the reveal.
- The count is one `querySelectorAll` over the list in one animation frame after a mutation that can change it: a `data-sar-kinds` write, a root filter write or a marked host leaving the page. One `MutationObserver` serves it and disconnects on `pagehide`; a page without a list surface gets no observer.

### Accessibility

- A real `button`, reached by Tab and pressed with Enter or Space. Its name is its text, and `aria-describedby` points at the line.
- A polite live region inside the notice repeats the line once it has held for one second, and a burst of new rows is announced once.
- The shared focus ring on `:focus-visible`, 7.53:1 on the bar's ground. In forced colors the notice gets a `CanvasText` edge, and the underline still marks the button.

### Don't

- Don't name tags or verdicts in the line: it counts titles only.
- Don't write the reveal to settings or storage.
- Don't scan for the count; read the marks the renderers wrote.
- Don't edit, restyle or join the store's bar, and don't reuse its classes.

## 17. Package summary

A block at the top of a package page (`/sub/<id>/`) that sums up the chips of the games the package includes, one line per tag, so a person sees what the package holds without reading every row: "AI Generated Content Disclosure: 2 of 5 games". It reuses the tag section's block and label (section 3) and draws from the verdicts the rows were looked up with: no request of its own (decision 57).

### When to use

- On a list surface named in `SUMMARY_PLACEMENTS` (`scan/placements.ts`): the package page's included games first. An entry is the same `ListPlacement` shape as the hidden results notice: the selector of the element that holds the hosts and, in `after`, the store element the block follows. A bundle page is one more entry once a capture shows its markup.
- One line for each kind that at least one included game shows a chip for: disclosed, not ignored for the game (`isIgnored`, through the capsule UI's `allowed`), enabled and with its badge switch on. A kind with no such game has no line, never a zero.
- With no line to show, there is no block and no label.

### What the consumer provides

```html
<div class="sar-tag-section sar-summary" role="group" aria-labelledby="sar-summary-label">
  <div class="sar-tag-section-label" id="sar-summary-label">t('badge_tags_title')</div>
  <div class="sar-summary-line">t('badge_summary_games', { kind, tagged }, count)</div>
  <!-- one line for each further kind, then t('badge_summary_pending', count) while some wait -->
</div>
```

- `tagged` counts the included games that show the kind's chip. `count` (the plural count) counts the included games whose verdict for the kind is known: an unknown game is neither counted as carrying the kind nor as not carrying it.
- While an included game has no answer yet, a last line says how many: "1 game not checked yet". It goes once every game has an answer.
- The summary is a renderer beside the badges (`createPackageSummary`). It keeps each host's last view in a `WeakMap` keyed by the host and counts the marked hosts inside the list in one microtask after a batch of draws. New verdicts, a retry's answer, a settings change and a change to the ignore lists reach it through the capsule UI's draws, with no observer, timer or request of its own.

### Look

- The tag section's block and label: `sar-text-summary-tag`, the label in `sar-color-text-muted` with `sar-space-1` below it. Each line is `sar-text-summary-tag` in `sar-color-text`, one per row, with no chip and no violet: the lines quote Steam's words and count games, and the rows below carry the chips.
- It goes right after `#package_header_container`, the package's title image, when the page has it, else right before the list of included games, with `sar-space-2` above and below. Without the list, nothing is drawn.
- One node of our own, inside no host, which keeps it out of every list filter's dim and hide.

### Accessibility

- The group is named by its visible label. The lines are plain text in reading order; the block is drawn once the answers are in and does not announce itself.

### Don't

- Don't write a zero line, "none" or "free of" for a kind: a kind no included game shows has no line.
- Don't count an unknown game in "of N".
- Don't send a package or bundle request for it: the rows' own lookups are enough.

### Limits that keep these true

- On the banner wash, text is `sar-banner-fg` only. `sar-color-text-muted` is 3.77:1 at the bright end, `sar-color-error` 4.21:1, and the store's own in-bubble link color `#c3c1c0` 4.49:1.
- The banner button's edge is white at 40%, the store's own, and 2.63:1 on our bright end. Its white label (8.06:1) identifies the button, so WCAG 1.4.11 does not ask 3:1 of the edge.
- `sar-color-text-subtle` only on page, row, panel, raised and sunken. On the store's row hover it is 3.32:1.
- `sar-accent-300` as link text only on solid grounds. On the store's row hover it is 4.45:1, so links there use `sar-accent-200`.
- The compact badge's surface stays at accent 900. A mid-tone flag color such as accent 700 would leave mid grey art with no boundary at 3:1 (surface 2.54, edge 1.11) and violet art at 2.01.
- None of this holds for a tag the person gave a color of their own (experimental): the tint, text, edge, surface and wash are mixed from the picked color, and nothing checks or adjusts them. The defaults keep every pair above; decision 49.
- The switch's off track is 1.55:1 against raised; its `sar-toggle-track-off-border` edge carries the 3:1.
